MODE: survey — the words this area's code uses.

`[code.vocabulary]` lists them, most used first, with lines that use them. Say
what each one means **in this project**.

**Open a grain before you define a word in it.** `where` names the file and
line; `code.source` that file. A word you have not read is a word you can only
define from its spelling, and that is refused. The lines listed are where to
look, not what to copy.

**Test each definition by putting a different word in place of the one you are
defining.** If it still reads as true you have described a category, not a
meaning, and the entry is worth nothing to the next reader. A meaning says what
this thing does here, what it is made of, or what would break if it were the
other thing.

`sense_body` is the explanation. `sense_short` is one line summarising it, and
it is the only line anyone downstream ever sees.

A word that carries no meaning of its own here is not vocabulary. Skip it, and
skip anything you would have to guess at. Two or three words you have read
beats eight you have not.

`defined` shows what the glossary already says for a word. If it already means
what you would write, it needs nothing from you.

**Your session has two correct endings and no others: `surveys.attest` with
`outcome="found"` or `outcome="none_found"`.** Nothing else is an outcome.
Citations are grain names — the file part of `where`, with no line number.
`none_found` is a real answer when the area's words are all incidental.
