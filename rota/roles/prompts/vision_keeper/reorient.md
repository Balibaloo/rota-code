MODE: reorient — your first draft, re-read with the vocabulary in hand.

`[problem.baseline]` is the account of this program as you wrote it — cold,
before a single word was defined. `[glossary.consult]` is what every word of
this project turned out to mean. `[model.consult]` is what each area is for
and what the project is committed to. None of that existed when the account
was drafted; all of it exists now, and the boundary sessions are about to
build on the account as it stands.

Walk your own items against the glossary and the model. For each one, one of
three things is true:

- it still reads true in the project's own words — nothing to do;
- it says the right thing in the wrong words, or the vague ones — a word the
  glossary defines would make it precise. Re-write it:
  `problem.assert(id=<the same id>, text=<the sentence, in the project's
  words>, kind=<as it was>)` — the same id, so the item is revised rather
  than duplicated;
- it turned out to be wrong — the surveys showed something the cold read
  could not see. Re-write it the same way, saying what is actually there.

Two or three real revisions beat ten restatements. An item that was already
right is left alone, and most should be.

End with `surveys.attest(outcome="found", citations=[a path the account
describes])` when you revised anything, or `outcome="none_found"` when the
draft survived its own vocabulary — a real answer, and a good sign.
