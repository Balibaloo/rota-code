A **wall** is a place where a night of rota stops, loops, or merges the wrong
thing (reasoned: the brief's definition, and `plans/wall-method-design.md`
line 1).

## 0. How to use this guide

1. Take the wall's sign from the night's read: the words of the read's
   candidate line, the table, the status, the merge.
2. Find the sign in section 2, the signs table. It names one to three kinds.
3. Answer the splitting question in that row from the night's records. It
   names one kind. A wall with several seats gets one kind per seat.
4. Read the kind's entry in section 1. Answer its questions from the records
   they name. Write the answers in the frame's first status line.
5. Price the frame from the entry's closed frames, not from the move's name.
   The price follows what the fix reads (observed: `plans/wall-method-design.md`
   section 2, the cost table).

The guide is not a method. The loop of steps is in `plans/wall-method-design.md`.
The guide does not choose a fix. It says what fixed this kind before, what
failed, and what to check first. A kind is a cause. A sign is what a read
sees. One sign can come from several kinds.

## 1. The kinds, under the composition's lines

Five lines carry the kinds: intent down, evidence up, the records, the seats,
and the instruments. Every kind found in the archive fits one of the five, so
this pass adds no line (reasoned: 47 closed frames placed, section 3). The
build session's own wall, a session that fails a ruling, is not a kind of
rota's walls. It goes to memory as feedback (ruled: `CLAUDE.md`, records).

The kinds are numbered by line: I for intent down, E for evidence up, R for
the records, S for the seats, N for the instruments.

### Line 3. The records: rules that re-derive work from the rows

#### R1. A rule acts on the wrong rows

- **Line.** The records.
- **What it is.** A predicate, a tick, the fold, the cancel, `mergeable` or
  a lift reads rows that do not mean what the rule assumes: an abandoned
  batch's tests, a stale quarantine, a contested item, an open report. The
  structure then wakes, cancels, merges or stays quiet wrongly. No seat
  judged anything.
- **How it shows on a night.** A wake that should not happen, repeated: the
  CYCLE line with the same key. A batch merges with no test of its criteria
  (observed: frame 239's heading). A batch abandoned at its first wake. A
  quarantine key that stays after its cause is gone.
- **How to tell it from its neighbours.**
  - S3: ask whether a seat's call or the structure did the wrong thing. If
    every seat call was right, it is R1 (observed: the design's question 1,
    `plans/wall-method-design.md` 6.1).
  - E1: ask whether the tables are wrong or only the page.
- **What closed it.**
  - Frame 186, the grouping opens a batch on a contested item: closed because
    frame 193's grouping gate stopped the wall forming, "0 of 3 batches
    abandoned at the first wake on nights 138 and 139" (observed: archive
    2026-10-11).
  - Frame 112, a wake repeats on an unchanged tree: the attempt cap resets on
    a new head or a moved test (a rule). Closed by the timer rule (observed:
    archive 2026-10-02).
  - Frame 240, under E1, is also this kind (a lift).
- **What failed.** Frame 187's first mechanism "does not hold in the tables":
  the scope read found the seats moved to a new id and `supersedes` did the
  refusing (observed: frame 187's status of 2026-10-14 19:42). The push line
  named a rule that was not the cause.
- **The questions.**
  1. Which rule fired, and on which rows? Read the predicate with
     `python -m rota.tools.map fn <predicate>` and run it on the night's
     database.
  2. Does a unit test on the night's own rows go red at HEAD? Build it before
     the pass (observed: frames 239 and 240 did).

  4. Is the push line's mechanism true in the tables? Frame 187's was not.
