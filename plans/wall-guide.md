# The wall guide: how to decide the fix of a wall

Frame 251, the wall guide. Designed 2026-10-15 on Fable by an agent of
session dae006d1, from first principles, with `plans/wall-research.md` and
its six tables as the evidence. Revised once the same day on the cold
review, `plans/wall-guide-review.md`. Every statement is about the rota
workflow, the seats, unless it says meta workflow, the build session. A
number copied from a record keeps that record's mark and date. Every frame
is named with its number and its name. Three words carry one sense each
here: a "sentence" of the composition, a "row" in a database, a "record"
file under `plans/`.

## 0. What this guide is for

Roman's purpose, in his words (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15, in the terminal,
`plans/wall-research/rulings.md` entries 7 to 13 and 43):

- "It's a guide to making decisions about how to fix walls. So night reader
  maybe, solution designer definitely."
- It makes two decisions easier: where the fix goes, by cause. Whether a
  fix overfits the night. Not how much to spend.
- Whether to fix at all is answered: yes, cheaply or by escalation. The
  morning's line, "if a wall is only a small inefficiency and we can't fix
  it easily then leave it ... we either can fix it cheaply (which is what
  the guide documents) ... or ... escalate all the way to the top" (ruled:
  Roman, 2026-10-15 10:51 UTC, superseded), gave way the same day to "I
  guess if we don't detect it then it's not that big of a deal, but ignore
  this, try to fix everything", then "Reasonably" (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15,
  in the terminal, the same minute). So the default for every wall a read
  names is a fix, cheap or escalated. A wall is not fixed only with a
  stated reason in its frame, and the frame stays open and visible on the
  stack. It is never dropped silently and never closed as left. "For now
  there is only cheap and escalate to me."
- Two walls are one kind by "the same cause in the structure".
- On overfit: "this is the kind of knowledge that we just have about the
  project."

The build session wakes cold, like a seat. Before this guide, the project's
judgement about walls sat in the 149 frames October pushed, the archive, the
decisions and the operating facts, and every hand-off relearned it
(observed: `plans/wall-method-fit-review.md` section 1, 2026-10-15). This
guide is the record the designer restarts from, so a wall costs one or two
cycles and not five to seven, and Roman sees only what is new (ruled: Roman,
2026-10-15, "Yes that's exactly it").

What this guide is not: a method, a ladder, or a price list. It reasons
about causes and the decisions each cause points to.

## 1. The structure, and the failures it permits

The composition is one page, and every cause below comes from one of its
sentences (observed: `plans/composition.md`, ratified 2026-09-16). A reader
rebuilds section 2 from this page.

**The parties.** The Principal is in the team. He holds intent, speaks,
writes no records, and his attention is the scarcest budget. The Liaison
faces him, infers his intent, and checks the reading. A seat is a small
model that wakes cold, acts once and ends. It reads only what the wiring
pushes into its prompt. The structure, not a seat, decides what happens
next. Capability comes from wiring. No brief grants what is not wired.

**The records.** Every record has one writer. Work is re-derived from the
records each pass. Nothing stops quietly. Conclusions cross a boundary, and
reasoning stays reachable by a pointer. A session commits or discards
before it ends.

**The flows.** Intent flows down: Principal, Liaison, records, seats.
Evidence flows up the same path. A prediction never gates. The tool never
rules for the Principal. Every claim cites a source.

**The ceiling.** The feedback rate is the ceiling. A weak model is trusted
only as far as the loop that checks it is short.

**The build session.** The same composition, a second instance: one strong
model in one context, about twenty cycles a sprint, that reads a night,
names walls and designs fixes.

From these sentences, what the structure permits at each place:

1. **At every hop of intent down**, a cold seat rewrites what came in. His
   words can be lost when the wiring has no slot for them, reversed,
   merged with what he declined, or paraphrased. The next hop reads the
   rewrite and not the words (reasoned: "Intent flows down"). Cause 1.
2. **At every seat**, the prompt is the whole world. Four causes: the fact
   is not in the prompt, because the wiring left it out or the window
   dropped it (cause 2). The fact is in the world and in no record, so
   nothing can push it (cause 3). The fact is in the prompt in a shape the
   small model does not read (cause 4). The seat reads it and judges wrong
   (cause 5) (reasoned: "Each seat wakes cold, acts once, and ends";
   "Capability comes from wiring").
3. **At every record**, a rule re-derives work from rows, and a row can be
   stale, another batch's, keyed on a value a seat can vary, or outlived by
   its cause (cause 6). Two records about one fact have two writers and no
   reconciler but the cascade (cause 12). A session's output that is
   neither committed nor discarded is a tree no record describes (cause
   14). A conclusion that crosses with no pointer is evidence nobody can
   check (cause 15) (reasoned: the composition, Records).
4. **At every instrument**, the wiring reads a reply (cause 7), admits or
   refuses a call (cause 8), tells the seat why (cause 9), and holds the
   mode's namespace (cause 10). A refusal's text is an instruction a small
   model follows literally (reasoned: "No brief grants what is not wired";
   `plans/wall-research/small-model.md`, limits C1 to C5).
5. **At the top of evidence up**, the page is the one surface he sees. A
   wrong page costs his attention and lands his answer on a wrong row
   (cause 11) (reasoned: "Evidence flows up the same path").
6. **At the measuring instrument**, a count from a wrong instrument is a
   prediction that wears the word observed (cause 13) (reasoned: "A
   prediction never blocks").
7. **At the build session**, the session names a wall from a status line
   and prices the fix by its own cycles. A door costs one cycle and a design
   ten, so the session reaches for the door, and its fix is the next wall
   (cause 16) (reasoned: the build session is a second instance).

The ceiling sentence sets the shape of every fix. A fix holds when it
shortens the loop that checks the seat or gives the next seat a fact. It
fails when it asks the same seat to judge better (reasoned: "A weak model is
trusted only as far as the loop that checks it is short").

## 2. The causes

Sixteen causes. Fifteen are rota's, and one is the build session's. Each
entry has the same fields. The October frames under each cause are this
pass's placement from the research's rows (reasoned:
`plans/wall-research/frames.md`, columns 3 and 4, re-read against the
structure, no turn read). Each "Cheap fix" gives one or two worked cases.

reach, the count of every place with the same gap (ruled: Roman,

one reader that covers all N places is the norm. A fix at one door of N

48 fixed one door of N at a median of 1 of 6 places. 45 of the 48 have a

"Not now, when" is not a decision to
leave a wall. It is the reason a session may write in the frame when the

## 3. Overfit, reasoned once

Roman: "this is the kind of knowledge that we just have about the project"
(ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15). This section writes it down.

**The one gating test is the count.** A door or a predicate carries, before
its pass, its count on every night's records: fires on a of n instances,
refuses b of N right calls, with the instances on the night as the
denominator. Rule C76 uses "reach" for every place with the same gap. The count
found frame 243's lexical door at 1 of 60 before any night ran (observed:
`plans/ladder-review-2026-10-15.md`, 2026-10-15). A door with no count is a
prediction, and a prediction never gates.

**The project's own rule is the sign to count first.** "Before you write a
door, ask: would this door refuse a correct act by a stronger model? If
yes, the check is a judgement. A judgement belongs in the brief and is
measured on the register. If the check names a state of the file or the
database that is wrong under every model, the check is a door" (ruled:
`plans/operating-facts.md` lines 304 to 305, 2026-09-09). The structure
says why. A seat is a stochastic text generator at the word level, and his
words are free text with no vocabulary. One word in the system text moved a
seat's act at temperature 0, and whitespace alone moved s173 (observed:
`small-model.md` G2; `plans/frame243-brief-loop.md`). So a predicate on
words samples one night's words, and a predicate on a state holds under
every model. Frame 234's door reads his words for the lines they reference,
a state of the text, and held at 0 false refusals. Frame 243's lexical door
read them for a rejection, a meaning, and failed at the count.

**Two models, a reasoned rule.** Every edit that corrected a defect helped
both models, and the only edit they disagreed on added emphasis. Helps
both: a fix. Helps one: emphasis. Helps neither: the diagnosis was wrong
(observed: `rota/DECISIONS.md` line 347). Roman's test is the same: "Are
any of the fixes specific to the language or model?" (ruled: Roman,
2026-10-05). The rule needs a run on the second model that no step
schedules, so a designer who applies it from one model's replay predicts
(reasoned: the review, 2026-10-15).

**Cost, not prohibition.** When two outcomes cost differently, noise decides
which one the seat takes. A fix that makes the right act cheaper than the
wrong one holds. A fix that forbids the wrong act leaves it cheap, and the
refusal's text teaches the next evasion (observed: `rota/DECISIONS.md` line
1248; `plans/operating-facts.md` line 318).

**Where the fix sits, a reasoned rule.** A fix at the hop that failed asks
the same seat to judge better. A fix one seat downstream gives the next
seat a fact from a record, which every seat that reads the record then
holds. The ceiling sentence says the second holds and the first does not.
The evidence is frame 243's record shape, which has no count until night
149 (reasoned: the ceiling; frame 243's two dropped rules).

**A brief sentence.** Measure it on the case that owns the behaviour plus
every case whose composed text holds the sentence, and on several replay
samples per seat before a re-record, because one replay is one sample
(observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, frame 82's and frame 234's lines;
`plans/frame243-brief-loop.md`). A change to the runner's shared prose owes
the whole register.

**The honest gap.** The doors table cannot decide overfit by door type: 1 of
8 phrase doors and 6 of 18 fact doors did not hold, and 17 of 26 have no
later count, 5 of them with no case since the fix (observed: `doors.md`).
The three phrase doors that failed did so at the count, before a night
(observed: `failed-fixes.md` rows 13, 15, 16). So the count catches an
overfit before the night does, and most doors are never counted again.
Count every door at every read, or its hold is a claim.

## 4. From a wall to a decision

Answer in order. Each question names its record. Stop at the first answer
that places the wall, and take that cause's entry. Steps 3 to 6 need no
interview. The interview is step 7's own step.

1. **Is the sign an open frame's sign?** `plans/stack.md`, the headings, and
   the night read's candidate line. Yes: a case added to that frame, placed
   by cause and not by sign. A wall whose sign matches but whose cause
   differs is a new frame (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-03, the residue rule). Two
   or more open frames under one cause become one design frame on that
   cause, which is how frames 131 and 190 closed (observed: the fit review
   section 2).
2. **Is the instrument right?** The control replay and the register's
   per-load history. No: cause 13, before anything else.
3. **Does the sign name his words or his page?** Then take this step before
   the others. His verbatim words, the verdict, the ruling, the criterion
   after each seat, the page. The first hop that differs: cause 1. The
   page itself: cause 11.
4. **Did a door fire, and was it right to?** The refusal in the next turn's
   `user` text, and the predicate's count on every night. Wrong to fire or
   wrong not to: cause 8. Right to fire and the step wrong: cause 9. The
   act has no call in the mode: cause 10. The reply held a readable call and
   none was recorded: cause 7.
5. **Did the rule read the rows it means?** The predicate, its query, and
   the rows. No: cause 6. Two records about one fact: cause 12. A tree with
   an uncommitted write: cause 14. A verdict with no pointer: cause 15.
6. **Was the fact in the seat's prompt?** The seat's turn in the night
   database. Absent: cause 2, or cause 3 when no record holds it. Present
   and the reply holds no call at all: cause 4.
7. **Present and read?** This step is the interview in the seat's exact
   context, one cycle. The seat did not read the fact: cause 4. The seat
   read it and judged against it: cause 5, and the ruling in cause 5
   applies before any escalation.
8. **Decide.** Three ends, beside the case added to an open frame in step
   1. The cheap fix with its count, when the entry has one and the count is
   in hand. The escalation to Roman, when the entry's "escalate" holds, as
   the problem at the composition's level, with the rulings the entry cites,
   so the question is only what is open. Or an open frame with its reason
   written, when the entry's "not now, when" holds: the frame stays on the
   stack, visible, and is never closed as left (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15,
   "try to fix everything", "Reasonably"). Before any question, two reads:
   what the records say, and whether the recommended answer holds against
   the frame's evidence (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-04 14:52).

**The reach question, before any pass.** When step 8 takes a fix, ask the
reach question of rule C76 (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-26,
`plans/method/reference.md` section 10). Count every place with the same
gap, not the cases at the place the night showed. Put the fix at the
smallest place that covers all of them. Write the reach count on the frame
before the pass, and the pass brief carries the count. A fix that covers
fewer places names the places it leaves and why. C76 holds the steps of
the read, and this guide does not copy them.
