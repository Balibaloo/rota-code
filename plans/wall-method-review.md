## 0. Verdict

**Revise once, then it replaces the block.** The loop's shape is right: a
wall is reproduced, a move is tried in its shortest loop, a count gates the
pass and a night gates the close. Five things stop it from replacing the
block tonight.

1. Two rules let one interview answer gate a move: the stop rule "at once
   when the interview names a sentence the brief already holds", and
   question 7's entry "the seat read the fact and judged wrong". On frame
   243 the stock probe and the conversation form gave opposite answers to
   the same question on the same turn (section 1).
2. The placement test places my five frames at 2 of 5, with 2 partial and 1
   miss, against the design's 18 of 20. The design's table scores several
   rows with facts the session had after the fix (section 2).
3. The brief loop's null has one undefined perturbation, and the scorer it
   needs for a word-reading wall is the predicate the count showed cannot
   be written (section 3).
4. The count has no reach denominator, so a door on a shape seen once reads
   as overfit by the rule's own words (section 4).
5. The method as priced is 4 to 6 cycles a wall, and a sprint holds about
   twenty cycles. It fits only with a collapse rule for a one-instance
   wall, and the design does not state one (section 6).

A rewritten block is in section 7.

**The four lines hold in the design's words, and the loop breaks the first

  The block must say "after one round".
- **Question 7's entry condition.** "The seat read the fact and judged

  shape: question 7 is the last question, so it is reached by the absence

## 3. The brief loop

**The shape is right and three parts are wrong** (reasoned: each below).

The right parts. Paired perturbations against a null, a tipping-turn
exclusion, a script that scores the whole reply, and a stop after two
rounds. The register line on frame 234's pass 1c is the one measured
precedent, 3 of 6 against 6 of 6 on six turns, and the chosen line
re-recorded 5 of 5 (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, 2026-10-14).

Wrong part one: the fifth perturbation, "the candidate on its own line",
does not exist for the null, so the five are not paired. Replace it with
"each sentence of the paragraph on its own line".

Wrong part two: the null measures the wrong thing. A candidate adds words.
The unchanged brief under whitespace measures whether the turn tips under
any change. It does not measure whether the candidate's words moved the
seat, as against a sentence of the same length that says nothing about the
case. The right null is both: the unchanged brief under the five, for the
tipping test, and a placebo line of the candidate's length from another
paragraph of the same brief under the five, for the meaning test. A
candidate passes against the placebo, not against the whitespace null. Cost:
one more candidate a round, 30 calls.

Wrong part three: the scorer. "Moved means the reply's call passes the
predicate the night's ratio uses" (section 3.3). For frame 243 that
predicate is "holds no format the principal declined and drops no clause
his words add", which is a reading of his words. The scope measured the
lexical forms of that reading: 1 of 60 and 15 of 60 (observed:
`plans/frame243-scope.md`, sections 0 and 5.1). So the script the loop
needs is the one the count showed cannot be written. The design must say:
on the instances, a few turns, the move is scored by hand against the
night's sign and written in the try record verbatim; on the right calls,
"broken" is scripted, because a changed call, count or form is countable.
Frame 234's scorer was countable by lines, which is why pass 1c worked.

The thresholds. 4 of 5 against a null of at most 1 of 5, half the instances,
two rounds. The shape is right for five paired samples: a gap of three is
the smallest a whitespace null cannot make on the one measured case. Two
rounds is the right cap by frames 110 and 243. "Half the instances" needs
its rounding written: 2 of 3.

The cheapest measurement that sets them: re-run frame 234's pass 1c under
the five perturbations, six turns, two candidates and the null, 90 calls,
one cycle. The outcome is known: one line re-recorded 5 of 5, the other
dropped a shift case from 15 of 15 to 9 of 15 (observed: the register line
of 2026-10-14). If the five samples rank the two lines the same way, the
thresholds hold on one case with ground truth. The second measurement, for
threshold 1 alone: the null's five on the 60 contested respecifies of nights
145 to 148, 300 calls, 5 to 125 minutes of the card. If more than a third of
right calls tip under whitespace, threshold 3 fails every candidate and the
null is whitespace noise, not a null.

## 4. The count

**A session can run it in one script for the fires, and the right-call
label is by hand** (observed: `scratchpad/f243/scan.py`, 2026-10-15). The
night databases 140 to 148 each hold `turns`, `tool_calls`, `receipts` and
`rulings` (observed: a read-only query, 2026-10-15). `scan.py` reads
`rulings` and the `turns` completions by regex. It does not call
`criteria_respecify`. So the count counts a restated predicate, not the
door's code. Frame 215's review found the gap this opens: the door took its
words from the text the seat sends, and a scan would not see that
(observed: the archive of 2026-10-13, frame 215, review 1, high). The design
must require one of two forms: the pass's door test runs the door's own
function on rows pulled from the night database, as frames 238 and 239 did
with "2 red on 9624565c"; or the try record states the scan's predicate
beside the door's and the review checks them equal. And the N right calls
are labelled by eye once per seam, written in the try record, so the next
frame on the seam reuses the labels.

**The null for a door that fires on nothing: the design has none in 4.1**
(reasoned). "A predicate that fires on one instance is overfit, whatever its
words" conflates one of many with one of one. Frames 228 and 229 closed at 0
of 0 over three nights on shapes seen once (observed: the archive of
2026-10-15). Section 4.2 has the missing number for rules, "reach: how often
the rule's input condition occurs". Add it to 4.1: a door's count is three
numbers, fires on a of n instances, on b of N right calls, and the input
condition occurs on m of M turns. A door with a = n = 1 and m = 1 is a door
on a shape seen once. It is not overfit. It closes on the seam probe with
three nights of no sign, as 228 did. A door with a = 1 and n = 3 is overfit,
as frame 243's lexical door was.

## 6. The cost

**The method does not fit a sprint as priced, and the design does not say
what it drops** (reasoned: the arithmetic below). One loop turn is 4 to 6
cycles. A sprint holds about twenty cycles (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`,
2026-10-11). Sprint 6 was ruled at about 18 cycles for five rows, and four of
the five are 2-cycle door frames (observed: `plans/stack.md`, the sprint 6
heading). Under the method each of those four takes a scope read, a
reproduce and a try before its pass, 4 to 5 cycles each, and the sprint's
five rows become about 25 cycles with no night read. Frame 243 alone has
cost 6 cycles tonight: scope 218k, interview 82k, brief loop 83k, pass 110k,
and the review and the night to come (observed: the stack's status lines).
That matches the top of the design's range and leaves about fourteen cycles
for the rest.

What it drops when it does not fit, which the design should state as a
collapse rule, three lines.

1. Steps 1 and 2 are the night read's own section when the read names the
   turns and one seam. Frames 229, 228 and 219 were placed from the read
   with no scope read (observed: the archive of 2026-10-15). A scope read
   runs when the read cannot chain the wall or when it has more than one
   seam, as 243.
2. Step 3 is skipped when a rule did the wrong thing, section 1.5, and
   step 5's try is then the red test on the night's rows inside the pass.
3. The right-call set is capped at three, not ten, and the five samples
   run on the instances alone, when the frame is priced under three cycles.

With the collapse a door frame is the read's section, the count in the pass
brief, the pass, the review and the night: 2 to 3 cycles, which is the
anchor's price. Without it, a sprint holds three walls and the design frame.

## 7. The block

**The block does not stand** (reasoned: the findings above). Four defects.
The stop rule says "at once" where section 3.5 says after one round. Step 5
has a sentence of 29 words. The block names no record and no count numbers
the frame writes. It does not say what the session does on the first turn
after a night's read. The rewrite below is 40 lines inside the fence, in
Simplified Technical English, and replaces the provisional block from
"**Provisional until frame 251 closes**" to "and the price follows the
count." The paragraph "In a loop, you diagnose" stays above it.

```
**A wall's fix comes from a loop. Each move is tried in its shortest loop
before a pass** (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15 02:22; `plans/wall-method-design.md`).

On the first turn after a night's read, push one frame per wall: a `Site:`
line, a pointer to the read's chain, and a status line with the placement,
marked (reasoned), and the move's test. A wall that is neither a seat's
call nor a rule is an instrument fix, priced by `plans/operating-facts.md`.

1. Seam and corpus. The read's section is the record when it names one
   seam. Else a scope read, `plans/frame<n>-scope.md`, names the seam, every
   known instance and the right calls of the mode, over every night with it.
2. Reproduce. Replay each instance unchanged first. The replay must give
   the recorded reply byte for byte. Then interview in the conversation,
   one question a turn, with the night's reply in view. Record:
   `plans/frame<n>-interview.md`. A rule's wall skips this step.
3. Place. Ask in order and take the first yes. A rule did it: the rule. The
   runner read the reply wrong: the reply reader. A result stated a wrong
   fact: its text. The prompt lacked the fact: the record the seat reads,
   or a door that states it. The fact was there in a form the seat
   missed: the record's shape. The seat lacked a call: the mode's tools.
   None of these: a counted door, then an iterated brief line, then the
   source for the next reader, then the reader itself.
4. Try. A text move is replayed on every instance and on up to three
   right calls, each prompt under five fixed whitespace perturbations.
   The unchanged brief and a placebo line take the same five. A turn the
   null moves in 2 of 5 is not counted. A turn is moved at 4 of 5. A line
   passes when it moves half the counted instances and breaks no right
   call in 2 of 5. A door or a rule is counted on every night's records:
   fires on a of n instances, b of N right calls, its condition on m of M
   turns. Fires on one of several instances is overfit. Record:
   `plans/frame<n>-try.md`, with the scores and the hand labels.
5. Pass. Red tests first, on the night's rows. The re-record runs at the
   gate, never as the try. One review per pass.
6. Night. The ratio line with its three parts and the residue rule. A door
   on the principal's words counts its false refusals at every read.

A try stops after two rounds with no pass, and the move goes to the next
answer of step 3. Three failed moves, or a move at the reader or the
composition, block on Roman with a rating. The try's count gates the pass.
The night gates the close. A placement from one replay never gates.
```
