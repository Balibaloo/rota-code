## 1. Where a refusal is born

**An op refusal reaches the seat as one `ERROR` line in the `feedback` list, and
`feedback` becomes the last block of `transcript`.** The path has five steps.

1. The seat's call runs through `sb.call(call.name, *call.pos, **call.args)` at
   `rota/core/runner.py:2577`, inside the `try` at `rota/core/runner.py:2575`

2. `Sandbox.call` validates the arguments first. A bad name raises
   `NotInWorkingSet` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:330`, and a bad argument raises
   `ArgumentError` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:339`. Then it calls the op wrapper at
   `rota/core/sandbox.py:341` (observed: `rota/core/sandbox.py:316-341`,
   2026-09-25).
3. The op wrapper runs `impl(ctx, **kwargs)` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:1996`. An
   op in `rota/roles/api.py` raises `ValueError`, or `Wall`, which is a subclass
   of `ValueError` at `rota/roles/api.py:261`. The wrapper appends
   `(label, str(exc), type(exc).__name__)` to `ctx.refusals` and re-raises
   (observed: `rota/core/sandbox.py:1995-2004`, 2026-09-25). The wrapper's own id
   guard also raises `ValueError` before `impl` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:1975`
   and `:1989`, and that path does not reach `ctx.refusals` (observed: the guard
   sits before the `try` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:1995`, 2026-09-25).
4. The runner catches every exception at `rota/core/runner.py:2814`. It appends
   `f"{call.name}: {exc}"` to `outcome.errors` at `:2815`, appends
   `f"ERROR {_call_tag(call, len(calls))}: {exc}"` to `feedback` at `:2820`, and
   adds `(call.name, str(exc)[:160])` to `turn_refusals` at `:2821` (observed,
   2026-09-25). `_call_tag` names the op alone on a one-call turn and adds its
   identifying argument on a multi-call turn (observed:
   `rota/core/runner.py:1496-1506`, 2026-09-25).
5. `feedback` is joined with `"\n"` and appended to `transcript` as the turn's
   results block at `rota/core/runner.py:2909`, after the seat's reply block at

## 2. How the next prompt is assembled

**The prompt is the transcript list joined with blank lines, after `_fit`, and
its tail is the last turn's results block.** The seat sees no other tail.

The transcript starts as `[user]`, the wake, at `rota/core/runner.py:2106`. Each
turn appends the reply at `:2858` and the results block at `:2909` (observed,
2026-09-25). Four special paths append their own reply-and-notice pair: the cut
reply at `:2310-2311`, code in the reply at `:2322-2323`, a named act at
`:2343-2344`, a no-op at `:2360-2361`, and the stood-by refusals at `:2394-2395`
(observed, 2026-09-25). So the order is: wake, then reply and results pairs in
turn order.

Inside one results block, the order is the order of the `feedback.append` calls
(observed: `rota/core/runner.py:2449-2907`, 2026-09-25):

1. `ERROR reply cut: ...` at `:2450`.
2. The fabrication notice at `:2467`.
3. One `OK` or `ERROR` line per call, in call order, `:2493-2820`.
4. `NOTE ... refused the same way in three turns` at `:2836`.
5. `NOT RUN ... before the answers ... came back` at `:2846`.
6. `NOT RUN ... tool result of your own` at `:2852`.
7. `You have sent your message ...` at `:2905`.

At the top of each turn, `transcript = _fit(transcript, budget)` at
`rota/core/runner.py:2243`, then `user = "\n\n".join(transcript)` at `:2244`
(observed, 2026-09-25). `_fit` rebinds `transcript`, so every change `_fit` makes
is stored and read again next turn (observed: the assignment at `:2243`; the
comment at `:2636-2638` calls the eviction notice "sticky", 2026-09-25).

`_fit` cuts in this order (observed: `rota/core/runner.py:649-707`, 2026-09-25):

1. A transcript that fits is returned unchanged at `:666-667`.
2. The wake is cut to two thirds of the budget at `:676-678`. `_render_cut`
   keeps the head of the wake and appends a `CUT your wake` notice.
3. With fewer than four blocks, `_cap_exchange` runs at `:686-687`.
4. With four or more, the head and the last two blocks are kept. Middle blocks
   are kept newest first while room remains, `:689-702`. The dropped blocks are
   replaced by `_dropped_note` after the wake, at `:707`.
5. `_cap_exchange` then cuts the last reply and the last results block when they
   do not fit, at `:598-630`.

`_exchange_cut` keeps `text[:keep]` and appends a `CUT this exchange` notice
(observed: `rota/core/runner.py:578-595`, 2026-09-25). **So a cut of the results
block removes its end first.** A line appended at the end of `feedback` is the
first line a cut takes (reasoned: `_exchange_cut` keeps the head).

The last 500 characters of a mid-session prompt hold the end of the last
results block: this turn's `ERROR`, `NOTE` and `NOT RUN` lines, or a
`CUT this exchange` notice (reasoned: the join at `:2244` and the block order
above). A refusal from an earlier turn is in the tail only when the seat sent
the same call again.

## 3. What "met" can mean in code

**No per-session structure tracks refused calls by key and clears them on a
later success.** The runner has five near misses.

- `ctx.refusals` holds `(label, why, class)` per op refusal, for the whole
  session (observed: `rota/roles/api.py:99` and `:138-139`,
  `rota/core/sandbox.py:2001-2003`, 2026-09-25). It is keyed by op name, not by
  call key. Nothing removes an entry when the op later succeeds (observed: no
  writer other than the appends at `rota/core/sandbox.py:2003` and
  `rota/core/runner.py:2701`, `:2739`, 2026-09-25). Ops read it to count earlier
  refusals, for example at `rota/roles/api.py:1405`, `:2789-2791` and `:7472`
  (observed, 2026-09-25).
- `refused_same` counts `(op, why[:160])` pairs per turn and never clears
  (observed: `rota/core/runner.py:2232` and `:2833-2843`, 2026-09-25).
- `already_run` holds the call keys that returned, added after the call at
  `rota/core/runner.py:2657` (observed, 2026-09-25). A refused call raises
  before that line, so its key is absent. A corrected call has other arguments
  and so another key (reasoned: `Sandbox.call_key` at `rota/core/sandbox.py:343-377`
  keys on the arguments).
- `served_keys` is per turn and counts evicted serves only (observed:
  `rota/core/runner.py:2448`, `:2808`, 2026-09-25). `repeated` counts repeat
  askings and is cleared by `forget` (observed: `:2186`, `:2221`, 2026-09-25).
  Neither one records a refusal.
- `ctx.writes` grows on every successful write op, and the runner reads it as
  "decided something" at `rota/core/runner.py:2891` (observed: the comment at
  `:2869-2889`, 2026-09-25).

So "met" can be one test today: `sb.call` at `rota/core/runner.py:2577` returned
without raising for the same op name. That is the unit the night 88 read used:
"a later turn ... shows `OK` for that op" (observed: section 6 of the night 88
read, 2026-09-25). A refusal that names another op as its cure has no code link
to that op (reasoned: the reason is free text in `str(exc)`).

## 4. Where a restatement would go so that it survives the cut

**Join the restatement to `user` at `rota/core/runner.py:2244`, after `_fit`, and
do not store it in `transcript`.** Pay for it by passing a smaller budget to
`_fit` at `:2243`.

The two lines today (observed, 2026-09-25):

```
2243            transcript = _fit(transcript, budget)
2244            user = "\n\n".join(transcript)
```

The change is, in outline (reasoned: from the five points below):

```
owed_block = _owed_lines(owed)                 # "" when nothing is open
transcript = _fit(transcript, budget - (len(owed_block) + 2 if owed_block else 0))
user = "\n\n".join(transcript + ([owed_block] if owed_block else []))
```

1. After `_fit`, no cut can reach the line. Inside the results block, the cut
   removes the end first (reasoned: section 2, `_exchange_cut` keeps the head).
2. Not in `transcript`, because `:2243` stores whatever `_fit` returns. A stored
   line becomes a middle block next turn. It then counts as the "reply" or
   "results" slot of `transcript[-2:]` in `_cap_exchange` at `:619` (reasoned).
3. `_prompt_overrun` at `:2248` measures `user` after the join, so an unpaid line
   reports "prompt over budget" into `outcome.errors` (observed:
   `rota/core/runner.py:710-746`, 2026-09-25).
4. The eviction test at `:2641-2643` reads `user`. A restatement that quotes no
   `OK` render and not `_EVICTED_PHRASE` leaves it unchanged (reasoned: the test
   looks for the phrase and for `answered[key]`).
5. The stored turn at `:2257` records the full `user`, so the restatement is in
   `turns.user` and in the cassette key (observed: `Turn(iteration, system,
   user, ...)` at `:2257`, 2026-09-25).

**What `_fit` reserves for the eviction notice.** On the four-block path it
subtracts `len(_dropped_note(len(transcript))) - 2` at `rota/core/runner.py:694-696`
(observed, 2026-09-25). That is the notice at its longest count, plus its
separator. The slack is at most the digits between the real count and the
worst case, a few characters (reasoned: the count only changes in its digits).
The three-block path at `:686-687` reserves nothing. **The reservation cannot
carry one more line.** A restatement needs its own subtraction, and a budget
passed in reaches both paths and `_cap_exchange` at once (reasoned: `budget` is
the only room figure `_fit` and `_cap_exchange` read).

Room inside `_cap_exchange` is floored at `_MIN_EXCHANGE`, 2,000 characters
(observed: `rota/core/runner.py:575` and `:618`, 2026-09-25). A large restatement
on a tight window can still overrun by the floor (reasoned). Cap the block, for
example at three ops and 160 characters of reason each, which is the same cut
`turn_refusals` uses at `:2821`.

## 6. Risks

- **The interview probe** quotes `outcome.user`, which is the wake only, set once
  at `rota/core/runner.py:2103` (observed: `rota/testkit/interview.py:69-88`,
  2026-09-25). It does not change (reasoned).
- **The repeat detection** at `rota/core/runner.py:2406-2417` compares the last
  three completions, not prompts (observed, 2026-09-25). The code does not
  change. The model's behaviour can change: a restatement is a new line in every
  prompt while the op stays open, and a seat that loops on reads may leave the
  loop to answer it (reasoned). That is the intended effect and also a new
  variable in every case with an open refusal.
- **The eviction test** at `rota/core/runner.py:2641-2643` reads `user`. It does
  not change if the restatement quotes no `OK` result and not the phrase
  `dropped to fit the window` (reasoned: section 4, point 4).
- **The fabrication check.** `FABRICATED_RESULT` matches a line that starts with
  `OK` or `ERROR` and a dotted name, in the seat's reply (observed:
  `rota/core/runner.py:510-511`, 2026-09-25). A restatement that starts with
  `ERROR op.name:` invites the seat to echo it, and an echo ends the turn's calls
  under rule 1 (reasoned). Start the line with another word, for example `OWED`.
- **The night counters.** `rota/tools/nightcount.py` matches the substrings
  `re-read anything you still need`, `unchanged since you asked`,
  `was run for you before you` and `OK code.write ->` in `turns.user`
  (observed: `rota/tools/nightcount.py:67-70`, 2026-09-25). A restatement that
  uses none of the four leaves the counts unchanged (reasoned).
- **`outcome.errors`.** Three readers read it, and `fixtures.py` reads a dotted
  prefix as a refused call (observed: the comment at
  `rota/core/runner.py:3103-3109`, 2026-09-25). Do not add the restatement to
  `outcome.errors` (reasoned).
- **False obligations.** 49 of night 88's 66 unmet pairs close the matter by
  design, for example "you have already sent report to liaison" (observed:
  section 6 of the night 88 read, 2026-09-25). No code field tells a closing
  refusal from a correctable one (reasoned: section 3). A restatement of a
  closing refusal tells the seat to do a thing the op will refuse again. Limit
  the set: exclude reads (`call.name in read_fns`), exclude the `"Repeat"`
  class, and drop an op once its `NOTE ... will not be accepted again` fires at
  `rota/core/runner.py:2834-2838` (reasoned). Even then, some closers stay in the
  set, so the wording must allow "end without it".
- **The frame's own constraint.** The line must not say "above" or "below".
  "Refused earlier this session" names a time, not a place (reasoned).
- **Cassettes.** Every recorded prompt after a turn with a refused write changes
  its key. The size is not counted here (reasoned: a count needs a replay).

## 7. Two candidate designs

**Design A: an owed list, joined after the cut.**

1. A runner dict `owed: dict[str, str]`, op name to the latest reason, beside
   `refused_same` at `rota/core/runner.py:2232`.
2. Set it in the `except` at `:2814` for a non-read op. Clear it after `sb.call`
   returns at `:2577`, and when the `NOTE` fires at `:2836`.
3. At `:2243-2244`, build one block of `OWED <op>: refused earlier this session:
   <reason[:160]>. Not done until a call of <op> returns OK.` Pass `budget` less
   its length to `_fit`, and join it after the fitted transcript.
4. Cost: about 90 characters plus the reason, at most 250 per open op per turn,
   and 0 when nothing is open (reasoned: 160 plus the fixed words). With a cap of
   three ops, at most 750 characters per turn.
5. Tests that move: none of the `_fit` unit tests, because `_fit` keeps its
   signature. New tests in `tests/rota/test_runner.py`. The cassettes of cases
   with a refused write, `ACT-TS-five-criteria-one-per-reply` first (reasoned).

**Design B: the owed lines at the head of each results block.**

1. The same `owed` dict and the same set and clear points as design A.
2. At `rota/core/runner.py:2449`, before any other line, insert the owed lines as
   the first lines of `feedback`.
3. `_exchange_cut` keeps the head, so the lines survive a results cut. They are
   stored in `transcript`, and `_fit` pays for them with no new code.
4. Cost: the same 250 characters per open op, but in every results block that
   stays in the window. Three kept turns with one open op cost about 750
   characters (reasoned: one copy per kept block).
5. Tests that move: `test_two_refusals_in_one_turn_are_noted_in_a_stable_order`
   at `tests/rota/test_runner.py:1394` if the owed line names the same op before
   the `NOTE`, since `find` then still works but the block changes. The same
   cassettes as design A, plus the length assertion in
   `test_asking_the_same_read_twice_does_not_pay_twice` at `:657-681` if a
   refusal is added to it (reasoned). The line is then not at the tail, which is
   the lever the frame names.

**Recommendation: design A.** It puts the line at the true end of the prompt,
after every cut, as the frame's lever asks. It costs one copy per turn, and it
leaves the stored transcript and `_fit` unchanged (reasoned: sections 4 and 6).
