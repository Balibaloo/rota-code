## Proposed LAWS changes

Each proposal is self-contained. The Principal rules on each one before it
lands. Identifiers, file paths and quoted terms are copied exactly. New text
follows Simplified Technical English.

### P1. Preamble (L1, lines 3–8)

Page: "LAWS derives from this page. This page does not derive from LAWS."
(page 3–4).

Old text:

> Every other document in this repo presupposes this one. `HANDOFF.md` opens at the
> laws; `schema.sql` opens at the tables; the prompts open at "You are Vision Keeper."
> Not one of them says what the whole thing *is*, which is why the vocabulary drifted
> in the first place — there was no level above the parts for the parts to hang from.
>
> Two parts, then. **L0**: what this is. **The laws**: what it may never do.

New text:

> This document derives from `plans/composition.md`. The page wins where the
> two differ. Every other document in `rota/` presupposes this one.
> `HANDOFF.md` opens at the laws. `schema.sql` opens at the tables. The
> prompts open at "You are Vision Keeper." This document has two parts.
> **L0** says what rota is. **The laws** say what rota may never do.

Reason: the old text claims there is no level above LAWS. The page is that
level.

### P6. The escalation ladder (L46, lines 178–185)

Page: "The Liaison is the one seat that faces the Principal." (page 25).
"Intent flows down: Principal, Liaison, records, seats. Evidence flows up
the same path." (page 69–70).

Old text:

> Developer → Architect → Vision Keeper → principal. Budget exhaustion escalates
> one rung; at the principal it is a page, a hold or a note, never silence and
> never a ledger row written on the principal's behalf (ruled 2026-09-13,
> `plans/principal-flow.md`). A cycle is bounded by the attempt caps and the
> ladder: a question nobody answers climbs (`unresolved`), a wake dispatched past
> its cap is quarantined and said out loud (`quarantined`). Nothing routes a
> counter-question into a suspended session; roles are single-instance, and the

New text:

> The ladder is Developer → Architect → Vision Keeper → Liaison. Only the
> Liaison faces the principal. Budget exhaustion escalates one rung. At the
> Liaison it is a page, a hold or a note for the principal. It is never
> silence. It is never a ledger row written on the principal's behalf (ruled
> 2026-09-13, `plans/principal-flow.md`). The attempt caps and the ladder
> bound a cycle. A question nobody answers climbs (`unresolved`). A wake
> dispatched past its cap is quarantined and said out loud (`quarantined`).
> Nothing routes a counter-question into a suspended session. Roles are
> single-instance. The next rung is a fresh session.

Reason: the old ladder reaches the principal with no Liaison. The page
allows one seat to face the Principal. Enforcement row 6 ("the top rung is
a page (`agenda`)") stays true.

### P7. Caps are rulings (L49, lines 195–197)

Page: "A ruling is a record. The Principal rules, the Liaison writes it,
and it carries a version." (page 62–63). Page 133 names the same misfit
for DECISIONS: "rulings live in config without a writer or a version."

Old text:

> Every cap is declared in `rota/config.py` and owned by the principal. A number
> written into a predicate is a decision made by whoever typed it, at a moment
> nobody remembers, that nobody can change without reading code.

New text:

> Every cap is a ruling of the principal. The ruling is a record with a
> writer and a version. `rota/config.py` declares every cap and points to
> its ruling. A number written into a predicate is a decision made by
> whoever typed it. Nobody remembers the moment. Nobody can change the
> number without reading code.

Reason: a cap owned by the principal is a ruling. The page makes a ruling a
record with a writer and a version. The old text puts the ruling in code
with neither. The mechanism (where the ruling record lives) is open. The
Principal rules on it with the DECISIONS change.

### P8. Amendment revokes (L59, lines 233–235)

Page: "A question changes nothing. Only a ratified amendment to something

Old text:

> Any write to an approved item drops it to pending and stops its batches.

New text:

> A ratified amendment to an approved item drops it to pending and stops
> its batches.

Reason: "Any write" includes an unratified write. The page denies that an
unratified change revokes. Law 9's own amendment (lines 100–103) and the
priority paragraph (lines 241–244) already exempt one class of write. The
rest of the paragraph is unchanged.

### P9. Preemption (L62, lines 246–250)

Page: "A seat restarts from records. There is no snapshot." (page 57).
"The snapshot design is dropped." (page 105).

Old text:

> **Preemption:** if a reorder bumps a batch ahead of the running one, the scheduler
> kills the running batch's environment outright — processes and ports die with it;
> half-dead environments are forbidden — and discards its checkpoint. The worktree
> and its commits persist as *deferred* work. Commit-first is what bounds the loss:
> an uncommitted change never existed.

New text:

> **Preemption:** if a reorder bumps a batch ahead of the running one, the
> scheduler kills the running batch's environment outright. Processes and
> ports die with it. Half-dead environments are forbidden. There is no
> checkpoint to discard. The worktree and its commits persist as *deferred*
> work. The batch restarts from its rows. Commit-first bounds the loss: an
> uncommitted change never existed.

Reason: the old text has the scheduler discard a checkpoint. Law 5 (lines
167–174) says no session writes one. The page says there is no snapshot.

### P10. Provenance `decided` (L68, lines 268–272)

Page: "They write no records. They speak, and the Liaison records." (page
19). "A record says whether it was observed from the code or the world, or
decided by the Principal." (page 58–59). "A ruling is a record. The
Principal rules, the Liaison writes it, and it carries a version." (page
62–63).

Old text:

> Entries in the model, glossary and problem statement are `decided` — the reason on
> file, written by the decider *in the same session as the decision*, with no
> recording step and no scribe role — or `observed`, extracted from an onboarded
> codebase: found, not chosen. Challenging an observed entry forces its first
> decision. The decision record accretes lazily.

New text:

> Entries in the model, glossary and problem statement are `decided` or
> `observed`. A `decided` entry rests on a ruling of the principal. The
> Liaison writes the ruling as a record with a version, and the reason is on
> file. An `observed` entry is extracted from an onboarded codebase: found,
> not chosen. A challenge to an observed entry forces its first ruling. The
> decision record accretes lazily.

Reason: the old text has a role decide an entry in its own session with no
scribe. The page allows three provenances only: observed from the code,
observed from the world, decided by the Principal. The page gives every
ruling a scribe: the Liaison. This proposal removes the role-made `decided`
entry. The Principal rules on that. The `cited` amendment (lines 274–282)
is unchanged.

### P11. Journals and identity (L83, lines 341–343)

Page: "A record's identity derives from its content, not from when it was
made." (page 60–61). "A ruling is a record ... it carries a version." (page
62–63).

Old text:

> * **Journals are exempt, by declaration.** The ledger, messages, decisions
>   record events; saying a thing twice is two events, and deduplication
>   would falsify history.

New text:

> * **Journals record events.** The ledger and messages key on the event.
>   Saying a thing twice is two events. Deduplication would falsify history.
>   A ruling in `decisions` is a record. Its identity derives from its
>   content, and it carries a version.

Reason: the page states the identity rule with no exemption. A ruling is a
record, so `decisions` cannot be exempt. This proposal keeps the event key
for the ledger and messages. That part still sits outside the page's rule in
a strict reading. If the Principal rules that every journal keys on content,
delete the bullet.
