## A. Every wall of October

`frames.md` holds 118 rows, one per frame pushed from a night's read from
2026-10-01 to 2026-10-15: 56 closed and 62 open (observed: the archive files
`plans/archive/stack-2026-10-*.md` and `plans/stack.md`, read by headings
and close lines, 2026-10-15). It leaves out 24 frames that a review, a gate,
a scene or Roman's word pushed, and the meta tooling frames. Section 5 of
the file names them.

The place of each cause is the sub-agent's placement, (reasoned: from the
frame's last status, not from a read of the turns). The counts:

- A door's predicate was wrong: 30.
- Other: 21. Of these, a door's text named a wrong fix, cause, call or fact
  in 9; a runner note stated a false fact or contradicted a door in 5; the
  page showed rows that are not his in 3; and 4 are single cases.
- A record misled a rule: 17.
- The runner or the parser read a reply wrong: 10.
- A seat's prompt lacked a fact: 8.
- Unplaced: 8 (frames 110, 115, 129, 150, 172, 181, 248 and 249). The
  record names the sign and not the cause.
- His words changed on a hop: 7.
- A mode lacked a tool: 6.
- The seat had the fact and judged wrong: 4.
- The project's runtime held a fact no seat saw: 3.
- The fact was in the prompt in a shape the seat missed: 3.
- The instrument that measures was wrong: 1.

The fix kinds of the 56 closed rows: a door 21, a runner change 13, a
predicate change 8, a design frame on a shared cause 5, a mode's tools 2, an
instrument fix 2, a record a seat reads 2, a brief line 1, not found 2
(observed: `frames.md` section 3). A brief line closed one October wall.

Four findings for the designer:

1. **The instruments make walls.** 30 causes sit in a door's predicate and
   14 more in a door's or a note's own text. So 44 of 118 walls sit in code
   that an earlier fix wrote (reasoned: the two counts; section B.3 names 16
   open frames whose heading names the earlier fix).
2. **His words carry few rows by cause and the costliest by price.** Seven
   rows place on a hop that changed his words and four on a seat that judged
   wrong. The fit review prices "the principal's words" at 7 to 10 cycles a
   wall, its most expensive row (observed: `plans/wall-method-fit-review.md`
   section 3, citing `plans/wall-method-design.md` section 2, 2026-10-15).
3. **The record often names the sign and not the cause.** 8 rows are
   unplaced, and the 62 open rows rest on the heading alone.
4. **Cycles are written only on closes from 2026-10-11.** Earlier closes
   carry none (observed: `frames.md`, "Not found"). The fit review's
   fourteen frames are the whole sample: about 89 cycles against about 43
   priced, median 5 (observed: the fit review section 2, 2026-10-15). The
   declined draft found three cycle counts that differ between the archive
   and the fit review, frames 234, 237 and 238 (observed:
   `plans/archive/wall-guide-opus-2026-10-15.md` section 5).

## B. What failed, and why in the record's words

`failed-fixes.md` holds three tables (observed: the archive lines each row
cites, 2026-10-15).

- **B.1, 17 rows.** 13 fixes were dropped, reverted or re-passed after a
  review, a re-record or a night. 3 premises or designs were dropped before
  a build (frames 111, 112 and 193). 1 suspect fix was cleared by an
  experiment (frame 101 against frame 88's brief).
- **B.2, 4 closes on a door that never fired** (frames 228, 229 and 110, and
  the draft's claim on 238 and 195).
- **B.3, 16 open frames** whose heading names an earlier door, nudge or
  brief line as the source of the sign.

What the failures share, in the records' words:

- **A brief line moves one case and breaks another.** Frame 234's line took
  the shift page "15 of 15 on the old prompt ... 9 of 15 on the new one", and
  the next line "re-recorded at 0 of 5". A runner sentence took a survey case
  "from 770/780 to 0/20 on llama" and "was reverted" (observed:
  `archive/stack-2026-10-15.md:22-23`; `plans/operating-facts.md:296`).
- **A brief line moves nothing when the seat holds the fact.** Frame 110:
  two brief shapes and a door before the interview. Frame 243: "no line moved
  both seats", and "two controls that changed whitespace only also moved
  s173" (observed: `archive/stack-2026-10-02.md:163`;
  `plans/frame243-brief-loop.md` section 0).
- **A wording fix applied everywhere is reverted, and the narrow form
  returns.** Frame 119's sentence was reverted after night 115. Frame 169
  rebuilt it only where the answer is gone, "because frame 119 removed it
  everywhere and had to revert". Frame 208 is open on frame 169's own match
  (observed: `archive/stack-2026-10-05.md:87`; `archive/stack-2026-10-11.md:134`).
- **A door on his words refuses right calls.** Frame 243's lexical door
  covered "1 of 60" contested respecifies. Its presence rule "refuses about
  59 of 60 right paraphrases". Frame 234's door was silenced by "one added
  sentence with no line number", in "74 of 131 replies". Frame 226's token
  set refused "the name the principal's own words ask for" (observed: rows
  13, 15, 16 and 11 of B.1).
- **A frame's premise is wrong at the scope read.** Frames 111 and 112
  (observed: rows 5 and 6 of B.1).
