## 0. The method in one page

A wall is a fact from a night: a batch stopped, a seat looped, or a merge
carries what the principal declined. The method is a loop of seven steps.
Steps 3 to 5 repeat inside one frame. Each step writes one record, and the
next step reads only that record.

| Step | Input | Output | Cost | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Chain | the night's records | the turns that carry the wall: each seat's call, the result, the commit | part of the night read | the night read's section |
| 2. Seam and corpus | the chain, every night that holds the seat's mode | the seam where the wrong thing first enters the build or the page; the corpus: each known instance, and the right calls at the same seam | a scope read, 1 to 2 cycles, 150k to 220k; the read's own section for a one-seam wall, 0.2 | `plans/frame<n>-scope.md` |
| 3. Reproduce | the corpus | for each instance: a control replay, byte-equal or not; an interview in the conversation; the class of the case (section 6) | 1 cycle, 70k to 105k; skipped for a rule's wall, 0.2 | `plans/frame<n>-interview.md` |
| 4. Place | the class | the first move, and the test that move takes | in the holding session, under 5k | the frame's status line |
| 5. Try on the case | the move, the corpus | a replay count or a records count: instances moved, right calls broken | 1 cycle a round, 60k to 100k, 3 to 63 minutes of the card for about 150 calls (reasoned: the review, point 3, two nulls) | the try record, `plans/frame<n>-try.md` |
| 6. Pass | the move that passed its try | the diff, red tests first, the re-record of the touched cases, the gate, a review | 2 to 3 cycles, 75k to 120k a pass, 50k to 95k a review | the pass report and the review file |
| 7. Night | the commit | the ratio line, read with its residue rule | 0 cycles, hours of the card, the night read | the night read, the frame's close |

A move that fails its try goes to the next answer of the placement test
and returns to step 5. A move that fails three tries, or reaches the reader
or the composition, blocks on Roman as a question (reasoned: the escalation
rule of `CLAUDE.md`, "a wall survives three cycles").

### 0.1 The move order, defined once

Two orders exist, and each has one job (reasoned: the review, point 1).

- **The order of the tries** is the meta loop that tests each move: a
  replay in seconds, a records count in minutes, a re-record in tens of
  minutes, a night in hours, a ruling in the principal's reply time. It
  says which test runs first. It never says which move wins.
- **The order of choice**, when more than one move passes its try, is the
  rota loop the move shortens. A door or a rule shortens the seat's loop to
  one turn. A result's text, the reply reader, a pin and the tools act at
  the next turn. A record's shape acts at the next wake. A brief line acts
  only through a re-record and a night. So: C or B first; then A, E, P and
  F; then D; then G; then H; then I. A brief line is the last move that a
  session makes alone (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15, "it's not as simple as
  adding a reminder", in the design brief; reasoned: the ladder review,
  section 6, the loop each move shortens).

### 0.2 The collapse rule

The loop as priced is 4 to 6 cycles a wall, and a sprint holds about twenty
cycles (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, the anchor of 2026-10-11). A
wall with one seam does not pay for every step (reasoned: the review, point
6).

1. Steps 1 and 2 are the night read's own section when the read names the
   turns and one seam. Frames 229, 228 and 219 were placed from the read
   with no scope read (observed: the archive of 2026-10-15). A scope read
   runs only when the read cannot chain the wall, or when the wall has more
   than one seam, as frame 243 had.
2. Step 3 is skipped when a rule did the wrong thing. Step 5's try is then
   the red test on the night's rows, inside the pass (section 1.5).
3. The right-call set is capped at three, and the five samples run on the
   instances and those three alone, when the frame is priced under three
   cycles.

With the collapse, a door frame is the read's section, the count in the
pass brief, the pass, the review and the night: 2 to 3 cycles, the
anchor's price for a door text (reasoned: the review, point 6).

### 0.3 The composition

The loop holds four lines of the composition.

- **A prediction never gates.** The class from one replay places the first
  move only. No rule gates on one interview answer: the answer flips with
  the probe's form (section 3.5). The try's count gates the pass. The
  night's ratio gates the close (observed: `plans/composition.md`, Flows;
  reasoned: the review, point 1).
- **The tool never rules for the principal.** A move that reads his words
  is admitted only when the count shows the reading holds on the records,
  and the count is re-read at every night read while the frame validates
  (section 5). Otherwise his words go to a reader: a seat that gets the
  words verbatim, or the principal on one line (observed: Flows; reasoned:
  the review, point 1).
- **A capability is judged by the loop it shortens.** The order of choice
  in 0.1 is that loop in rota (observed: The ceiling; reasoned: the review,
  point 1).
- **The feedback rate is the ceiling.** Step 5 exists so that a candidate
  meets its shortest loop before it meets a re-record or a night (ruled:
  Roman, 2026-10-15, "maybe we can iterate briefs on the specific case
  before doing a full re-record").

## 1. Reproduce

### 1.1 What the replay is

A replay sends one recorded turn, `turns.system` and `turns.user`, to the
seat's own model with the session's pins, as one `/api/chat` call (observed:
`plans/operating-facts.md`, the anchors, "A replay of one turn is one
`/api/chat` call with those two strings"). Three tools exist.

1. `probes/frame37/interview_turn.py`: replays one turn with no tools, adds
   a preamble and every question in one call. Arguments: `--db`, `--sid`,
   `--seq`, `--questions`, `--model`, `--think`, `--timeout`, `--host`
   (observed: the file's arguments at HEAD).
2. The conversation form, `scratchpad/f243/interview_conv.py` of session
   4754aaec: call 1 replays the turn, the night's own completion goes in as
   the assistant turn, and each question is its own user turn (observed:
   `plans/frame243-interview.md`, section 1). The same session's
   `subst_replay.py` replays a turn with one brief sentence replaced.
3. The seam probe, `seamlib.py` in the scratchpad folder `seam135` of
   session c81b16d3: it sends a recorded turn with the real door's refusal
   in place of the night's result, reads the next call, and sends that
   call through the doors (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, the line
   "A door that no night meets closes on a live seam").

Tools 2 and 3 live in scratchpads. The method needs them in `probes/`, so
the first frame this design pushes is their port (reasoned: a step of the
loop must not depend on a dead session's folder).

### 1.2 The control comes first

Replay the unchanged turn before any question or candidate. The replay must
give the recorded completion byte for byte. Three cases are on record.

- Frames 110, 243 and 165: the control reproduced (observed:
  `plans/frame110-interview.md`, "The replay matched byte for byte";
  `plans/frame243-interview.md`, section 1, s188 and s173; the frame 165
  review's replay, 9 of 10 sessions equal to the byte).
- Frame 237: the control did not reproduce. s187's turn 1 matched 80
  characters and then wrote `code.source` calls where the night wrote a
  prose list, 3 of 3 runs (observed: `plans/frame237-interview.md`, section
  5; `plans/operating-facts.md`, the probe line of 2026-10-14).
- Frame 135's seam probe: the control held at 3 of 4 turns (observed: the
  seam line in `plans/operating-facts.md`).

### 1.3 What the replay proves, and what it cannot

A replay that reproduces proves that the turn's prompt alone gives the
wrong call. It then proves, per candidate, whether a changed prompt gives
another call on that turn. It is one sample: the pins hold temperature 0,
so five runs are one call (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, "A sample
of five is one live call").

The replay cannot prove five things (observed or reasoned as marked).

1. **Spread.** One prompt hash gave 4 glossary words and later 6 at
   temperature 0 (observed: the register line of frame 237, pass 1c). Two
   replays of s173 that changed whitespace only, and no word, moved the
   seat to a right call (observed: `plans/frame243-brief-loop.md`, section
   1). One replay that moves is not a rate. Section 3.3 takes several
   samples for that reason.
2. **A cut.** The probe sends no `num_predict`, so a turn the night cut at
   the output budget does not cut on the replay (observed: the probe line
   of 2026-10-14; the gate line on the cut paths).
3. **The next turns.** A replay of turn 1 changes the result that turn 2
   would read. Only the seam probe replays a turn after a changed result
   (reasoned: each turn's prompt holds the earlier results).
4. **The chain's effect.** Whether the batch merges, cancels or loops
   depends on other seats and on rules (reasoned: the wall of frame 243 is
   s188's text read by s190, two sessions apart).
5. **Another card.** A recording made on the Titan differs from one made on
   the 3080 at temperature 0 (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, frame
   124). A replay runs on the card the night used.

### 1.4 The interview

Ask in the conversation form, one question a turn, with the night's own
completion in view. The stock probe asks every question in one call with no
completion, and on frame 243 it gave answers the record contradicts: s173
said "I did not write the words 'default spaced'" (observed:
`plans/frame243-interview.md`, section 1). Ask about inputs, never about
reasons alone (observed: the probe's docstring, the rule of
`rota/testkit/interview.py`). Four questions place a case:

1. What did the prompt say about the thing the call got wrong? Quote it.
2. Did you read that as a fact for this call, and which line?
3. Which line of your instructions told you to do what you did?
4. What one sentence would have moved you? Then write the call with it.

Answer 4 is a candidate for step 5, never a fix. On frame 243, s188 named a
sentence its brief already held, and under that sentence it sent the same
text byte for byte (observed: answers 4 and 5).

### 1.5 When a case has no replay

- The wrong thing was written by a rule, not by a seat: a fold, a cancel, a
  merge, a lift, a tick. Frames 225, 239 and 240 (observed: the archive).
  The case is reproduced by a unit test on the night's rows, red at the
  parent.
- The control does not reproduce: frame 237. The interview is still read
  against the record, but no replay count gates the pass. The move takes a
  records count or goes to the night.
- The wall needs the next turn after a result: use the seam probe.
- The wall sits at the principal's reading or at a ruling. No replay
  exists, because the reader is a person (reasoned).

## 3. Iterate on the case

This is the loop Roman asked for (ruled: Roman, 2026-10-15, "our briefs are
not perfect, maybe we can iterate briefs on the specific case before doing
a full re-record"). It serves moves A, B2, D, E, P, F and G. One precedent ran it
and it worked: frame 234's pass 1c replayed two candidate lines on six
recorded turns at no GPU cost for the register, ranked them 3 of 6 against
6 of 6, and the chosen line then re-recorded at 5 of 5, after a first line
re-recorded untried had dropped a shift case from 15 of 15 to 9 of 15
(observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, the line on frame 234 pass 1c).

### 3.1 The set of turns

1. **Instances**: every known instance of the wall at this seam, over every
   night that holds it, each a turn whose control reproduces. Frame 243 has
   three: s188 on night 148, s130 and s173 on night 147 (observed: the
   scope, section 3).
2. **Right calls**: recorded turns of the same mode where the seat did
   right, at least as many as the instances, at most ten. For frame 243:
   contested respecifies with right text, from the 60 on nights 145 to 148
   (observed: the scope, 4.1).
3. **Register cases**: every case whose composed text holds the changed
   text. List them before the try, not after (observed: the register line
   on `ask.md`, "list every case whose composed text holds the line").

### 3.2 The measured fact the round is built on

The loop ran once on frame 243, before this design, and its result sets the
round's shape (observed: `plans/frame243-brief-loop.md`, 2026-10-15 02:27,
eight lines, sixteen replays, one sample per seat per line, temperature 0).

- No line moved both seats. Line 1 moved s188 and half of s173. Line 5, the
  keep sentence removed, moved s173 and not s188 (section 0 of the report).
- Two controls that changed whitespace only, no word, moved s173 to a right
  `cj_compact` (section 1 of the report).
- Line 1 moved s188 in the loop. The same words with one line break inside
  them did not move s188 in the interview (section 0 of the report;
  `plans/frame243-interview.md`, section 4).

So one replay at temperature 0 is one sample, and a whitespace change is
enough to move a seat that sits at a tipping point. A move on one replay
says little about a line's meaning (reasoned: the three facts above). The
night also runs at temperature 0, and its prompts differ in their working
set from wake to wake, so the spread over small perturbations is the
spread the night meets (reasoned: `plans/operating-facts.md`, "Sizes go up
and down between the turns of one session").

### 3.3 One round

1. Write one to three candidates. Take each from the interview's answers and
   from the brief's own wording, never from a phrase of the night's
   sentence (reasoned: a line that names a wrong word pulls the seat to it,
   `plans/operating-facts.md`, frame 234 pass 1c). A removal of a sentence is
   a candidate too: line 5 of frame 243's loop was the only line that gave
   s173 a fully right call (observed: the report, section 4).
2. Write two nulls (reasoned: the review, point 3).
   - **The whitespace null**: the unchanged brief. It measures whether a
     turn tips under any change.
   - **The placebo**: a sentence of the candidate's length, taken from
     another paragraph of the same brief, put where the candidate goes. It
     measures whether the candidate's words moved the seat, and not only
     the added length.
3. Make five samples of each candidate and of each null. A sample is the
   prompt with one fixed perturbation that changes no word: as written; the
   paragraph's line breaks made spaces; two newlines after the paragraph;
   one newline before it; each sentence of the paragraph on its own line.
   The same five serve every candidate and both nulls, so the samples are
   paired (reasoned: the report's two controls are two of these five; the
   pins hold temperature 0, so a repeat of one prompt is no sample,
   `plans/operating-facts.md`, "A sample of five is one live call"; the
   fifth perturbation of the first draft did not exist for a null, the
   review, point 3).
4. Replay every sample on every turn of 3.1, items 1 and 2, at the
   session's pins.
5. Score the replies in two ways (reasoned: the review, point 3).
   - **Instances, by hand.** "Moved" means the reply no longer shows the
     night's sign. For a wall that is a reading of the principal's words,
     no script can score this: the scope measured the lexical forms of
     that reading at 1 of 60 and 15 of 60 (observed:
     `plans/frame243-scope.md`, sections 0 and 5.1). The agent scores the
     few instance replies against the night's sign and writes each reply's
     call verbatim in the try record beside its score.
   - **Right calls, by script.** "Broken" means the reply changes its call,
     its count or its form against the recorded right call. That is
     countable. The whole reply is scored, not the target call alone:
     frame 237's candidate moved the form 3 of 3 and wrote 54 calls where
     the brief asks for a few (observed: `plans/frame237-interview.md`,
     section 5); frame 243's line 1 moved s173's `cj_compact` and lost
     "TypeError" from `cj_serializable` (observed: the report, section 3).
     Frame 234's scorer was countable by lines, which is why its pass 1c
     worked (observed: the review, section 3).

### 3.4 The thresholds

1. **A tipping turn is not counted.** A turn whose whitespace null moves in
   2 or more of 5 samples sits at a tipping point. It leaves the count for
   this round, and the try record names it. On frame 243, s173 is such a
   turn (observed: the report, section 1, 2 of 2 perturbations moved it).
2. **A turn is moved** when the candidate moves it in 4 or more of 5
   samples, and the placebo moves it in at most 1 of 5 (reasoned: a gap of
   three is the smallest that a whitespace null did not make on the one
   measured case; the review, point 3).
3. **A right call is broken** when the candidate breaks it in 2 or more of
   5 samples.
4. **A candidate passes** when it moves at least half of the counted
   instances, rounded up, so 2 of 3, breaks no right call, and at least one
   instance is counted. The bar is a ratio and never a zero, by the ruling
   of 2026-10-03 on a stochastic seat (observed: `CLAUDE.md`, the three
   parts of an ends-when; reasoned: the review, point 3, for the rounding).

The thresholds are (reasoned) until a measurement sets them. **The cheapest
measurement** re-runs frame 234's pass 1c under the five perturbations: six
turns, two candidates and the null, 90 calls, one cycle. Its outcome is
known: one line re-recorded 5 of 5, and the other dropped a shift case from
15 of 15 to 9 of 15 (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, the register line
of 2026-10-14). If the five samples rank the two lines the same way, the
thresholds hold on one case with ground truth. A second measurement, for
threshold 1 alone, runs the whitespace null's five on the 60 contested
respecifies of nights 145 to 148: 300 calls, 5 to 125 minutes of the card.
If more than a third of the right calls tip under whitespace, threshold 3
fails every candidate, and the whitespace null is noise and not a null
(reasoned: the review, point 3).

### 3.5 When the loop stops and the move goes up

- **Two rounds**, six candidates, with no pass. The move goes up (reasoned:
  frame 110 tried two brief shapes and a door over five passes before the
  record shape moved it, `git log` 7da1d435 to e7cd1b39; frame 243's loop
  ran eight lines and found none that moved both seats, the report,
  section 4).
- **At once**, before any round, when every instance is a tipping turn or
  the control does not reproduce. Both are facts of the replay, not answers
  of the interview. The replay cannot measure the case (section 1.5).
- **After one round, never at once**, when the interview named a sentence
  that the brief already holds and no candidate moves that seat in 4 of 5.
  The interview's answer alone gates nothing: on s188 the stock probe
  answered "Yes, I read the principal's words as rejecting" and the
  conversation form answered "No" to the same question (observed:
  `plans/frame243-interview.md`, sections 2.1 and 3.1; reasoned: the
  review, point 1). The round's count is the fact that stops the line.

"Goes up" means the next move the placement test names for the case
(section 6), not the next row of the table. On frame 243 the loop's own
reading is that a brief line does not answer s188 (observed: the report,
"Reading for the frame").

### 3.6 The re-record, and when it runs

The re-record runs once, in the pass, after a candidate passes. It is never
the try. The pass runs the touched test files, then the gate, and the gate
re-records its stale set one case at a time (observed:
`plans/operating-facts.md`, the gate lines from frame 89). Its cost: about
15.7 seconds a case live; the 9 Liaison cases about 45 minutes of the card;
runner prose about 128 live calls, tens of minutes (observed: the same file,
the re-record lines). A register case that goes red under the passed line
is a broken right call that the try missed. Add its turn to the set of 3.1
and run one more round before the pass continues (reasoned: the set grows
with each miss, and the next wall's try starts from it).

### 3.7 The price of a round

One cycle: an agent on Opus, 60k to 100k, writes the candidates, the
placebo and the perturbations, runs the replays and the score script, scores
the instances by hand, and writes the try record (reasoned: frame 243's

## 4. The count

### 4.1 For a check or a rule, moves B1 and C

The test is the ladder review's, with a third number (observed:
`plans/ladder-review-2026-10-15.md`, section 3; reasoned: the review, point
4). **Run the predicate on the records of every night that holds the seam.
Write three numbers:** it fires on a of n known instances; it fires on b of
N right calls; its input condition occurs on m of M turns of the mode.

- **Overfit** is a < n with n > 1: the predicate fires on one instance of
  several. Frame 243's lexical door fired on 1 of 60 contested respecifies
  and reached 1 of 3 cases (observed: the scope, 4.1 and 5.1).
- **A shape seen once** is a = n = 1 with m = 1. It is not overfit. Such a
  door closes on the seam probe with three nights of no sign beside it, as
  frames 228 and 229 closed at 0 of 0 over three nights (observed: the
  archive of 2026-10-15; reasoned: the review, point 4).
- **A cue list** that fires on many with b = 0 is not overfit. Frame 234's
  doors refused 13 to 20 over-contests and 0 right rulings on five nights
  (observed: the ladder review, section 3).
- b must be 0. The count reads the nights that hold the seat's mode, not
  only the nights that hold the wall. Frame 243's count read nights 145 to
  148 (observed: the scope, the sources).

**The count runs the door's own code, or proves its scan equal** (reasoned:
the review, point 4). `scratchpad/f243/scan.py` reads `rulings` and the
completions by regex and does not call `criteria_respecify`, so it counts a
restated predicate (observed: the review, section 4). Frame 215's review
found the gap this opens: the door took its words from the text the seat
sends, and a scan would not see that (observed: the review, section 4, the
archive of 2026-10-13). So one of two forms holds:

1. The pass's door test runs the door's own function on rows pulled from
   the night database, as frames 238 and 239 did with "2 red on 9624565c".
2. The try record states the scan's predicate beside the door's, and the
   review checks them equal.

The N right calls are labelled by eye once per seam and written in the try
record, so the next frame on the same seam reuses the labels.

A door also has a step. Follow each sentence of the refusal exactly on the
replay, because a refusal that names a next call is a route (observed:
`plans/operating-facts.md`, the frame 135 line: four reviews each found a
hole behind a sentence). So a door's try is the count plus a seam probe on
the instances: the seat's next call after the refusal.

### 4.2 For a move a replay cannot test

The equivalent of the count has three parts.

1. **Red first on the case.** A unit test built from the night's rows is
   red at the parent and green after. Frames 238 and 239 did this (observed:
   the archive, "2 red on 9624565c").
2. **Reach.** Count over every night's records how often the rule's input
   condition occurs, and how many of those the change alters. Frame 243's
   record-shape candidate counts the declined merges where the builder's
   prompt lacked a ruling with words: 3 of 3 (observed: the review, section
   5). A reach of one, on a shape seen once, is not overfit by itself. It
   closes on its red test with three nights of no sign beside it (reasoned:
   the review, point 4).
3. **No right path changed.** Of the occurrences the change alters, name
   each one that was right on the night. That number must be 0.

A move that neither a replay nor a count can test, H's principal line or I,
has no equivalent before the night. Its price is the principal's attention,
and it is a ruling, so it is a question to Roman with a rating (observed:
`CLAUDE.md`, the rating rule).

## 5. The night

The ends-when line keeps its three parts (observed: `CLAUDE.md`, the ruling
of 2026-10-03).

1. **The mechanism**: what the move meets, which the pass builds and the
   review checks. For a replay move, the try's count is part of it.
2. **A ratio with the baseline of the night that found the wall**, never a
   zero. Frame 243: merges that carry what the principal declined, from 1
   of 3 on nights 147 and 148 toward 0 of the next 3 (observed: the scope,
   4.5).
3. **The residue rule**: a session that still shows the sign is read, and
   the frame closes when its cause is not the cause the frame named. That
   cause is a new frame. Frame 234 closed this way, its residue m81 went to
   frame 243 (observed: the archive, frame 234's close).

Two night rules apply to the loop.

- A door that no night meets proves its mechanism on a live seam probe
  (observed: `plans/operating-facts.md`, frames 135 and 136). The probe is
  the ends-when's first part, never its ratio. The close also carries three
  nights of no sign beside the probe, as frames 228 and 229 closed "on the
  door test, because the door never fired in three nights" (observed: the
  archive of 2026-10-15; reasoned: the review, point 1).
- A door that reads the principal's words re-reads its false-refusal count
  at every night read while the frame validates. The count was taken on his
  past words, and his next words are new (reasoned: the review, point 1;
  observed: frame 234's 0 right rulings refused on five nights, counted by
  hand).
- A night that loops on another frame's wall is stopped (observed:
  `CLAUDE.md`, the ruling of 2026-10-09 20:19).

When the night's ratio fails and the residue's cause is the frame's own
cause, the loop returns to step 3 with the night's new instances added to
the corpus. The move that failed on a night goes up, by the same rule as a
failed try.

## 6. The placement test

### 6.1 The test

Ask the questions in order, from the facts the session holds at that
moment: the chain, the seam, the control replay and the records. The first
yes names **the place to look**, not the move. The move at that place can
be the one that makes a missing fact exist, as on frames 215 and 195
(reasoned: the review, point 2).

0. **Is the wall a seat's call or a rule at all?** If no, it is an
   instrument wall. It leaves the loop at step 1 and is priced by
   `plans/operating-facts.md`. Frame 235, the client's connect to
   `localhost`, is such a wall (observed: the archive of 2026-10-14).
1. **Did a rule do the wrong thing, not a seat?** A fold, a cancel, a merge,
   a lift, a tick, a wake, or a door that refused a right call. Then move C
   at that rule.
2. **Did the runner read the reply wrong?** No call, a list, a code block, a
   call in prose, an invented result, or a form the parser drops. Then move
   E, with move G for the call's written form. The words match move E's row
   (reasoned: the review, point 2, frame 232).
3. **Did a result the seat read state a wrong fact?** Then move A, tried on
   the replay. A result that stated no fact is question 4, not this one
   (reasoned: the review, point 2, frame 215).
4. **Did the seat's prompt or result lack the fact the right call needs?**
   - The fact is in a record: move D when the seat must read it before it
     acts, move B1 when the fact is checkable at the call.
   - The window or the output budget cut the fact: move P.
   - Nothing produces the fact: move C at the producer (reasoned: the
     review, point 2, frame 195).
5. **Did the prompt hold the fact in a form the seat missed?** The record
   shows it: the fact sits inside an escaped string, past the window's
   middle, or at the end of an error line. Change the form where it sits:
   move D for a wake or a page, move A for a result. The interview can
   point here. It never decides it alone (reasoned: the review, point 1).
6. **Did the seat lack a call, or hold one it should not, or fail to state
   one the op needs?** Then move F, or B2 for the op's signature.
7. **None of the above.** The seat had the fact in a readable form and the
   call is wrong. Try in this order: a door, counted by section 4.1, move
   B1; then a brief line, iterated by section 3, move G; then the source for
   the next reader downstream, move D at that reader; then move H. When
   more than one passes, the order of choice of 0.1 decides. This question
   is reached by the absence of a yes on 0 to 6, never by the interview's
   claim that the seat "read the fact and judged wrong" (reasoned: the
   review, point 1).

A case with several seats is placed per seat, at the seam where the wrong
thing first enters the build or the page (observed: frame 243's scope, seam
1, "the build reads the criterion and nothing else"). A design frame over
several walls is placed per wall (reasoned: frame 193 below).
