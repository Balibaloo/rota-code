## The picture in one paragraph

One turn is: rota runs, the build session reads the run, the build
session changes rota, and rota runs again. Today a turn is closed on
itself. The turn asks one question: did the last fix move its own count
at its own place? The answer is almost always yes, so the session
judges "continue". The read then names about ten problems, and more
than half of them are old. Each problem gets a fix for the one case
that was seen. The fix adds machinery, and later problems can come
from that machinery. No step of a turn looks back at what
returned, or at what the earlier fixes caused. Roman sees it from
outside and turns the work. Two things are true together. The count
that the nights measure rose: nights with a merge went from 8 of 33 to
20 of 33 and 19 of 32. And the problems returned at one rate through
the same weeks. The picture must hold both.

## The reading of the whole, after all the reads of the day

(reasoned: this session.) The project cannot tell a good turn from a
bad one, on either side.

- On the rota side, each turn changes many things at once: the code,
  and often the requests, the seat, the start or the harness. The noise
  of a run is not measured. So a count moves after almost every turn,
  and no move can be tied to a change.
- On the side of the problems, no counted end is a clear end, and most
  ends have no recorded cause.
- On the side of the build session, the judge of a turn is the fix's
  own count, and it says "continue". Roman's look is the only thing in
  the records that finds a miss.

So the records cannot say that the project can spiral well, and they
cannot say that it cannot. They say why it cannot see. A turn can tell
only with the same conditions on both sides of one change, and more
than one run on each side. None of the 107 turns had that.

## One turn, step by step

Each count is (observed: the page that the line names). Each page had
one reader.

1. **Before the run, a question exists, and it is about the last fix.**
   88 of 91 runs with a read carry a line such as "frame N validates on
   night X". Two runs ask about the whole: dry run 1 and blind run 1
   (the list of turns).
2. **The run counts a merge, and no person sat in the seat.** A script
   approved every page up to night 132. From night 133 a model sat in
   the seat and contested, and 39 contests cancelled a batch. Only
   blind run 1 scored against a key, 1 of 72 designed runs. The noise
   of a run is not measured: the records hold no pair of equal runs
   with a fresh start (the product map; the list of turns).
3. **The read names more problems with time.** About 6 for each read in
   the first weeks, then 9, then 13. A read is the build session's own
   account, and 36 turns hold a kind of stop that no read named (the
   list of turns).
4. **The first act comes 1 minute after the read**, as a median. After
   a hand-off between sessions it comes after 2.5 minutes. The speed
   is a fact and no cause: Roman corrects a fast first act as often as
   a slow one (the list of turns; the boundary page).
5. **A fix comes in for one seen case.** The first solution was the
   right size in 0 of 15 cases. `rota/roles/api.py` went from 25
   refusal sites to 421, and the code holds 503 today. Of 40 drawn
   doors, 31 came in for one seen case. 25 of the 40 are general rules,
   so a door is not always a fix at one place (the meta synthesis,
   finding 1; the product map).
6. **The close rests on the fix's own count.** Of 46 long problems that
   ended, a count confirms the end for 18. In 10 of those 18 the change
   is at the place of the sign. After the end, 7 of the 18 came back or
   showed at the same place under another name, 11 are "not sure", and
   none is a clear no (the read of the chains).
7. **The problems return at the same rate.** More than half of the
   named problems came back in the next three reads: 55%, 61% and 61%
   over eight weeks of nights, and 48% in the dry runs. The shares are
   an upper reading, because a second look split 19 of 41 long chains
   (the list of turns; the read of the chains).
8. **A fix of one turn can be a problem of a later turn.** The count is
   for the problems that returned over four turns or more, a chosen
   set, and not for all 789 named problems. Of 54 such problems with a
   found origin, 37 come from code that an earlier fix added. 28 of 46
   ends have no recorded cause: a read stops naming the problem (the
   read of the chains).
9. **The session judges "continue", and Roman turns the work.** The
   work continued in 46 of 49 turns that a session judged. Roman turned
   15 of the 18 turns where the work turned (the list of turns, counted

10. **Between sessions the words cross, and the act does not follow

    Roman's look finds that. He looked at 48 of 93 hand-offs, and he

## Why the problems return at one rate

(reasoned: this session, from the ten steps.)

- Something did build up. Nights with a merge rose, the sessions for
  each merged batch fell from 906 to 404 and 260, and the last third
  had a stricter seat. The batches that wait behind a stuck batch fell
  from 25 to 1. The give-ups of the Developer stayed near 30 in each
  third. No read looked for the changes behind those counts.
- Each step is correct by its own rule. The fix passes its test. The
  status line is true. The frame closes on its count.
- Every test in a turn is the test of that turn's own step. A fix is
  tested by its count, at its place, for about three runs.
- Nothing tests the turn as a whole. No step asks whether rota is
  nearer the destination, what came back, or what the last fixes
  caused.
- So the only view of the whole is Roman's. He gave "fix at the cause
  and not at one door" six times in four weeks (the meta synthesis,
  finding 1).

## What a turn would need, to build on the last one

(reasoned: this session. These are needs that the reads point at. They
are not a design, and no build follows from this page.)

- A judge of "continue or turn" that is not the fix's own count and is
  not Roman each time. This is the one need that the boundary reads
  support. A judge that is not Roman exists at the scale of one diff:
  the diff review found 7 faults in pass 1 of the put-down tool. No
  such judge exists at the scale of a turn. That sentence is not
  tested.
- A measure of a run with known noise. Without it, a difference between
  two runs can be the change, the noise or both.
- Three records at a step: a question before the run that is about the
  destination or about what came back; a look back in each read; and a
  recorded end for a problem. Each one can be needed, and none is
  enough. A record that a session holds does not bind its act: 16 of 26
  corrections, and step 1 shows the same, because 88 of 91 runs have a
  question and the turn is still closed on itself.

## What the picture does not know

- **Where a turn went well.** The records cannot name one. The read of
  the measured counts finds 46 moves that hold, and 14 of them reverse
  inside six nights. 20 lie at a change of the conditions, and 21 of
  the other 26 have such a change inside their own eight nights. A
  count moves in the next three runs after 73 of 85 turns, so a change
  before a move is near certain by timing alone. A rise exists, and
  nothing in the records ties it to a change
  (`plans/method/moved-count-read-2026-11-10.md`, one reader, grades
  by a script).
- **Why a session acts against words that it holds.** The read of the
  16 misses gives the moment and not the cause. The thinking of the
  model is empty in 13 of 16 records, so a silent recall cannot be
  told from no recall. One more record at a step is the kind of thing
  that was in view in 8 of the 16 misses.
- Whether a person can run rota alone and read the result. No run
  tested that (the product map).
- Whether a stronger model at the Developer's desk moves the stop.
- How soft the links between problems are. One reader made each link,
  and "not sure" is the largest class of many cells.
- Whether this is Roman's meaning of "spiral well". Its test is that he
  finds his meaning in it.
