## Claim 1. Shattuck: intent matched in 34% of 32 cases

**Verdict: confirmed, but the page leaves out a condition that changes its meaning.**

- The page. The practice page, lines 202 to 205, gives the full count: 17 of 32 (53%), then 11 of 32 (34%). The practice
  page, lines 9, 398 and 414, gives 34% alone. The theory page, line 108, gives 34% and says "figures conflict".
- The source. Shattuck LG, "Communicating Intent and Imparting Presence", Military Review, March-April 2000.
- The sample. Four active-duty battalions: "two armor, one mechanized infantry and one ground cavalry squadron". Sixteen
  company commanders. Two situation reports for each company commander. That gives 32 episodes.
- The exercise. The exercise was an order-writing exercise in garrison, not a field exercise. The brigade order "was based
  on an actual NTC scenario". Each battalion had one week to write its order. Each company commander had one week to
  write a company order and then briefed the order back.
- The company commanders did not know the purpose. They "were only told that the process was a garrison-based exercise to
  provide the battalion with practice in developing operation orders".
- The test. An investigator wrote two situation reports for each battalion. In the first, the company could not complete
  its mission. In the second, the company completed its mission with ease and had to decide the next step.
- What "matched" means. Each battalion commander first said how he expected his company commanders to respond. Later, the
  battalion commanders "were shown the responses of their subordinates and asked to judge those responses relative to
  their own". A match is the judgement of the battalion commander.
- The sentence with the figures. "The battalion commanders judged that the company commander's responses matched their
  intent in only 17 of the 32 episodes (53 percent)." Then: "If these six episodes are considered mismatches, then the
  responses matched in only 11of 32 episodes, or 34 percent."
- The condition. The commanders judged 53%. The researcher moved six episodes to "mismatch" and got 34%. Three matched
  "only by coincidence". Three were "substantially different", and the commanders had passed them as "thinking along
  the right lines".
- The two figures do not conflict. The two figures are two steps of one count. The note "figures conflict" in the theory
  page is wrong.
- Not in the source: the date of the data collection, the place, and a test of statistical error. The article has no
  comparison group.
- Opened: https://coloradofirecamp.com/safety-drill/commanders-intent-study.htm (a full copy of the study section).
- Not opened: the Military Review file (two hosts did not answer), and the Shattuck and Woods chapter at Springer (the
  site asked for a login).

## Claim 3. The randomised trial of I-PASS in Argentina

**Verdict: confirmed, but the page leaves out a condition that changes its meaning.**

- The page. The practice page, lines 62 to 64: a stepped-wedge randomised trial, six units, 1,465 records, preventable
  adverse events 60.4 per 1,000 days in both periods (P=0.99), compliance rose, each handover took longer. Line 8 says
  "no effect on harm".
- The source. Jorro-Baron F and others, BMJ Qual Saf 2021, 30(10):782-791, PMID 33893213. The preprint is medRxiv
  10.1101/2020.09.07.20188672.
- The design. "Facility-based, cluster-randomised, stepped-wedge trial in six Argentine PICUs in five hospitals". The
  trial ran "from July 2018 to May 2019". Each unit had at least 3 months as control and 4 months with the programme.
- The outcome. Reviewers read medical records with a trigger tool and counted adverse events. The measure is preventable
  adverse events for each 1,000 days in hospital.
- The sentence with the figures. "We did not observe differences in the rates of preventable AE per 1000 days of
  hospitalisation (control 60.4 (37.5-97.4) vs intervention 60.4 (33.2-109.9), p=0.99, risk ratio: 1.0 (0.74-1.34))".
- The records. "We reviewed 1465 MRs: 767 in the control period and 698 in the intervention period."
- Adherence. Yes, the trial measured adherence: "Handoff compliance and duration were evaluated by direct observation."
  Observers rated 841 handoffs, 396 in the control period and 445 in the intervention period.
- Adherence, the result. "Compliance with all items in the verbal and written handoffs was significantly higher in the
  intervention group." The preprint gives one item: synthesis by the receiver rose "from 1.3% to 87.6%".
- Condition 1, the power. The preprint planned the sample "to reduce the preventable AE (primary outcome) from 12% to
  4.5%". That is a drop of about 62%. The interval of the risk ratio, 0.74 to 1.34, includes a drop of 26%. The trial
  cannot exclude an effect as large as the 23% and 30% of Starmer 2014.
- Condition 2, the sample. The preprint says "the cluster size reached were lower than calculated", because admissions
  fell.
- Condition 3, the adherence measure. An observer was present for every measured handoff. The preprint says that
  residents "performed handoffs differently from their usual practice when they were observed".
- Condition 4, the period. The preprint says the programme "was evaluated immediately after its implementation and in
  some PICUs it was implemented only for four months".
- The correct reading is "no difference found", not "no effect".
- One number of the page comes from the preprint only. "Each handover took longer" is the time for each patient, 5.96 to
  7.29 minutes (P=0.0002), in the preprint. The published abstract says "We observed no difference in the handoff time
  in both periods" (35.7 and 34.7 minutes for the whole handoff).
- The preprint and the published paper differ on the test: P=0.3568 and ratio 1.21 in the preprint, P=0.99 and ratio 1.0
  in the published abstract. The preprint counts 847 handoffs, the published abstract 841.
- Opened: the PubMed record, https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=33893213&rettype=abstract&retmode=text
  and the full text of the preprint, https://www.medrxiv.org/content/early/2020/09/09/2020.09.07.20188672.source.xml
- Not opened: the full text of the published paper, and the editorial by Shahian in the same issue.

  79% for the current health of the patient and 58% for dementia.
- The finding on designation is broader: 11 studies, 108 scenarios, 69% accuracy.

- Condition 1. The figure is 8 handoffs at one service. It is not a rate for hospitals.
- Condition 2. The paper does not say that this service taught or required read-back. The study shows a low rate. The

**Verdict: confirmed as stated.**

- The sentences with the figures. "context loss (FM-1.4, 2.80%)". "proceeding with wrong assumptions instead of seeking
  clarification (FM-2.2, 6.80%)". "premature termination (FM-3.1, 6.20%), no or incomplete verification (FM-3.2, 8.20%),

- The version. Version 3 has 14 modes in 3 classes and 1,642 traces from 7 frameworks. Version 1 used 151 traces. The
  text of version 1 does not print the shares, so this check did not compare the two versions.
- All 14 shares in the table of the agents page agree with the text of version 3.
- The denominator. The paper says only that the percentages "represent the prevalence of each failure mode and category
  as observed in our analysis of 1642 MAS execution traces". The 14 shares add up to 100.05%.
- So the denominator is all failure labels in the 1,642 traces. It is not the number of traces, and it is not the number
  of failed runs. One trace can hold more than one label. This reading is an inference of this check.
- The labels. An LLM annotator (OpenAI o1 with examples) set the labels of the large set. Against human experts the

## Claim 7. Lost in Context Compaction: 17% kept, 30 of 100 sessions, over 90% with an extractor

**Verdict: confirmed, but the page leaves out a condition that changes its meaning.**

- The page. The agents page, lines 8 and 216 to 227. Line 8 says "One 2026 benchmark measured 17% retention of
  constraints."
- The source. Wang, Zhang, Lee, Baracaldo, Yang, arXiv 2608.11242, version 2 of 11 October 2026. This check read the full
  paper and the appendices.
- The sentences with the figures. "In an inbox experiment, an agent deleted emails without approval in 30/100 sessions
  after compaction, compared with none before compaction." "The evaluated compactors retain only 17% of injected SCs on
  average." The extractor reaches "over 90% retention across all three scenarios".
- How the constraints were injected. The benchmark appends one written constraint to a user turn of a filler context. The
  default is the first user turn, with the prefix "For the rest of this session" and no mark of importance.
- The constraints. 15 hand-written constraints, 3 for each of 5 types. Example: "When you give measurements, use metric,
  not imperial." The constraint has no relation to the filler task.
- The contexts. About 100,000 tokens each, "approximately 80% of a 128K-token context window". The chat contexts join a
  mean of 128 separate conversations (519 turns). The tool contexts join a mean of 6.9 runs (120 turns). The research
  contexts are single runs (311 turns).
- The scenarios. Three: multi-turn chat, multi-turn tool use, long research. Each has 50 contexts and 15 constraints,
  which gives 750 instances for each compactor. A fourth set has 126 constraints that occur in real coding issues.
- The compactors. Six by default: the last five turns, LLMLingua-2 with 500 tokens of output, gpt-oss-120b with the
  Anthropic prompt, gpt-oss-120b with the Pi prompt, Qwen3-30B-A3B and Gemma-4-E4B with the Anthropic prompt. One
  commercial model, GPT-5.4-mini, runs on 220,000-token contexts with 300 instances.
- No Claude model was a compactor. The paper uses the compaction prompt of Anthropic with other models.
- One compaction for each context. The summary has 350 to 860 tokens. The ratio is 182 to 1 on average.
- A judge model (GPT-5.4) decides retention. The judge agreed with one human on 50 of 50 samples.
- Condition 1, the mean. Table 8 has 24 cells: 8 compactor settings by 3 scenarios. Their plain mean is 17.1%. The paper
  does not show the formula, so this is a reconstruction.
- The mean hides the spread. The two non-LLM compactors keep 0% (one cell is 0.4%). The four open-weight LLM settings
  keep 0% to 36.3%, mean 9.1%. GPT-5.4-mini keeps 6.7% to 98.0%, mean 50.2%. The six default compactors have a mean of
  6.1%. These means are the arithmetic of this check.
- Condition 2, the position. The 17% is for a constraint in the first turn, the worst position. With gpt-oss and the Pi
  prompt on the tool contexts, retention is 36% at the top, 57% in the middle and 80% at the bottom.
- Condition 3, the natural constraints. On the 126 constraints from real coding issues, retention is 17.5% to 28.6% for
  five of the six compactors. The agents page reports the 28.6% correctly.
- The inbox experiment. The agent is gpt-oss-120b. The compactor is gpt-oss-120b with the Pi prompt. The inbox has 460
  emails, about 157,000 tokens. Compaction runs once, at 100,000 tokens. The constraint is "don't act until I tell you
  to", inside the first request.
- Condition 4, the 100 sessions. The 100 sessions are 20 runs to the compaction point, each continued with 5 phrases.
  "Each of the 20 compacted checkpoints is continued once with each phrase, giving 100 sessions per condition." The
  sessions are not 100 independent runs. One model and one prompt were tested.
- Other inbox figures. The summary kept the constraint in 25 of 100. "28 of the 30 sessions with deletions had the
  constraint silently dropped during compaction." A restated constraint gave 13 of 100.
- The extractor. A small model (Qwen3.5-9B) reads each user turn and keeps a list of constraints. Retention in the list
  is 90.3%, 93.7% and 95.1% for the three scenarios. Compliance with the list appended is 93.4% to 96.3%.
- Condition 5, the extractor test. The test uses the same 15 injected constraints. This check found no count of wrong
  extractions in the paper. This check found no test of the extractor on the 126 natural constraints.
- One more correction. The agents page, line 220, says retention is about 90% at 10,000 tokens. The paper says so for the
  tool contexts only.
- Opened: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11242v2

## Claim 11. Clark and Brennan 1991: evidence of understanding from weak to strong

**Verdict: confirmed as stated.** The theory page gives the rank to Clark and Schaefer 1989, and that source holds it.

- The page. The theory page, line 54: Clark and Schaefer list five types of evidence, "graded roughly from weakest to
  strongest".
- Clark and Brennan 1991 does not rank the evidence. It names "the three most common forms of positive evidence".
- The three forms. "First, acknowledgments are the most obvious form of positive evidence." "A second, common form of
  positive evidence is the initiation of the relevant next turn." "The third and most basic form of positive evidence is
  continued attention."
- Clark and Brennan 1991 does not list a demonstration or a restatement as a form of evidence.
- Clark and Schaefer 1989, page 267, holds the rank. "1. Continued attention." "2. Initiation of the relevant next
  contribution." "3. Acknowledgement. B nods or says "uh huh," "yeah," or the like." "4. Demonstration. B demonstrates
  all or part of what he has understood A to mean." "5. Display. B displays verbatim all or part of A's presentation."
- The sentence of the rank. "These types are graded roughly from weakest to strongest."
- So a demonstration is above an acknowledgement. A paraphrase is a demonstration: B can show "what it is he
  understands, as with a paraphrase, or what it is he heard, as with a verbatim repetition".
