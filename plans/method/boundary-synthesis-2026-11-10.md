the words pass. It is lost between a session that holds the words and
the same session's act.

- What a note holds does not change the share of takers that Roman
  corrects.
- Of 26 corrections by Roman after a take, 21 repeat a thing that he

The reads do not show why a session acts against words that it holds.
That is the open question of this research.

## The first result, kept as it was written at 14:15

My hypothesis was wrong in its main part. I said that the goal is lost
at a hand-off because the note carries the next act and not the
question. The project's own records do not support that. What a note
holds does not move the share of takers that Roman corrects.

The five reads point at three other things. Each one decides whether
the next mind holds the goal.

1. The place. A thing that was said once survives when it stands in one
   lasting place apart from every summary. It is lost when it lives in
   a conversation or inside a summary.
2. The first acts. A receiver acts at once, and nobody checks its first
   acts against the goal. The sender is gone or silent, and a receiver
   that asks Roman cannot be reached by anybody.
3. The judge. Only action on real cases, judged by the one who knows
   the goal, shows that the goal arrived. Roman is that one. Most
   hand-offs get no such judgement at all.

The say-back that I proposed is a real practice and a weak proof. It
finds differences at low cost. It does not show that the purpose
arrived.

## The question

What must cross a boundary so that the next mind holds the goal, and
not only the next act? Roman's doubt set it: "I'm not sure that we know
how to communicate between sessions even directly".

## What each read gave, in one paragraph

Read 1, the channel. 24 messages went among five sessions today. An
idle session saw a message in under 2 seconds, and a working session in
2 to 23 seconds. A session that waited on a question dialog to Roman
saw nothing until he answered. Rota-3d has waited on such a dialog
since 12:27 and saw neither message sent to it. A sender cannot tell a
read message from an unread one.

Read 2, our own hand-offs. In 32 hand-offs, 14 notes state a goal or a
question and 18 do not. Roman corrected the taker inside three hours in
4 of the 14 and in 5 of the 18. The taker's first edit, commit or agent
came at a median of 2.55 minutes after the take. The taker wrote to the
sender in 22 hand-offs, and the sender wrote back in 5. Roman typed
nothing in 13 of the 32 windows. 55 of 151 hand-off receipts have no
note.

Read 3, other fields. Medicine, aviation, the military and shift
operations share a live exchange with questions, a fixed list of
topics, an active receiver, a visible moment where the responsibility
passes, and a check after that moment. No field shows that the purpose
arrives. In one paper exercise, battalion commanders judged that 17 of
32 acts of their company commanders matched their intent, and the
researcher's stricter count was 34%. In one hospital service, receivers
read back in 8 of 48 observed handovers.

Read 4, theory. Seven conditions: delivery, shared words, the goal
above the tasks, the current state, the unwritten part, ownership, and
the known gaps with a way to repair them. An observer can check
delivery and state. No check confirms the others for certain. The
weakest evidence is silence, then a receipt, then "I understand", then
a restatement. The strongest is correct action over many real cases,
judged by a mind that knows the goal, with no warning. No theory says
how much evidence is enough.

Read 5, published work on agents. Direct loss of history is 2.8% of
the failures of multi-agent systems, and failures of verification are
23.5%. In one study, summaries of a long session kept 17% of the
constraints that the user stated once in the first turn, as a mean over
24 set-ups that run from 0% to 98%. A list kept apart from the summary
kept over 90%. A second study gave a taker the repository alone, the
first agent's notes, or its raw trace. Each kind of context cut the
taker's effort, and the paper says that no kind wins on every measure.
No source measures whether a receiver understood before it acted.

## The three things, each with its evidence

### 1. The place

- Outside: a mean of 17% against over 90%, for a summary against a list
  kept apart (read 5). The 17% depends on the summariser. The weakest
  kept 0% and one strong model kept 98%.
- Theory: a goal is a thing above the tasks, and a task list does not
  determine its purpose (read 4, condition 3).
- Here: Roman asked the root question at 00:26, at 11:38 and again
  after 12:45, to three sessions. It stood in no note before 12:25.
- Here: the page of Roman's words, `plans/frame287-roman-words.md`, has
  no line after 2026-10-17. The place exists and nothing feeds it. His
  words since then are in transcripts, in memory files and in status
  lines of frames.
- Here: a frame's heading holds the goal of the frame, and the stack
  carries it across every hand-off. That fits read 2: most of the 32
  hand-offs carry one frame, and the note's content does not matter for
  them. A goal above the frame has no heading. The read of 114 units
  above a frame found the same gap from the other side.

### 2. The first acts

- Here: the first act comes 2.55 minutes after the take (read 2).
  Rota-82 started a build three minutes after its take today, on a note
  that was wrong.
- Here: the sender answers in 5 of 22 cases (read 2). A brief rule
  tells the sender to keep the end of its context for the taker, and
  the records show that the exchange is rare.
- Here: the ruled way to ask Roman is a dialog, and a session with an
  open dialog sees no message (read 1). Rota-3d did what its note said
  and is out of reach.
- Outside: nobody checks is the large class of failure, 23.5% against
  2.8% for lost history (read 5).
- Outside: every field puts a check after the pass, by an overlap or by
  a review from the sender (read 3). I had no such step.

### 3. The judge

- Theory: the strong evidence is action on cases that nobody arranged,
  judged by the one who knows the goal (read 4).
- Outside: where a field tested the purpose on new cases, about half
  of the receivers missed it, in one paper exercise (read 3).
- Here: Roman is the only judge, and he typed nothing in 13 of 32
  windows (read 2). Today he judged every session, and each one needed
  corrections. So a low count of corrections in the records can mean an
  absent judge, and not a taker that understood.
- Here: this joins statement 5 of the meta picture. The work moves what
  the run measures, and no measure of the destination exists. A session
  with no judge steers by the count that it has.

  The channel is sound. The dialog closes it.
- Hypothesis 1, the note carries the act and not the question: not
  supported. 14 of 32 notes state a goal, and it changes nothing that
  the records can show.
- Hypothesis 2, a say-back finds a difference: held today, twice. It is

- Hypothesis 3, a session that holds the question changes its act: one

  the order of read 4.
- My recommendation of a centre page written by me: I wrote that read 5
  speaks against a sender's account as the carrier. The check of the

  the notes. So the outside work neither supports nor refutes a centre

## The join with the picture of the project

The same three things stand at the boundary between two runs of rota.

- The place: the target is on `rota/COMPLETION.md`, which no frame
  cited for six weeks. Session rota-a4 says that a run has no question
  written before it.
- The first acts: a read of a run names frames, and the next run starts
  before those frames are built. Nobody checks the first act after a
  read against the read.
- The judge: a merge is counted with a seat that agrees, and equal
  nights spread from 0 to 3 merges. No run is judged against the
  destination.

So one reading covers the two sides. A turn builds on the last turn
when the goal has a place that outlasts the turn, when the first acts
of the next turn are checked against it, and when somebody who knows
the goal judges the result on real cases. Today all three rest on
Roman. That is my reading of why the project spirals badly, and it is
his cost. It is a reading. Its test is whether he finds his meaning in
it.
